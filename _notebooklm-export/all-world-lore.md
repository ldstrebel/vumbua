# The Circuit-Run

> *"I also hear there is a RESO-RACE coming after the first week of classes! A perfect place to prove your metal to the Captains."*
> — [[Valerius Sterling]], radio broadcast

The **Circuit-Run** (colloquially: **Reso Race**) is the most popular sport in Harmony. It is a brutal combination of exploration race, engineering competition, and religious simulation of the "Great Stitching" — the ancient conquest that unified Harmony's territory.

---

## Core Concept

Teams or soloists pilot custom craft (**Venture-Rigs**) through a wilderness arena, racing to **strike Spires** (simulated nodes) and siphon their energy. Once a team has accumulated enough resonance to overcome their craft's energy handicap, they must reach the **Grand Resonator** at the arena's centre and discharge everything. The first team to light the Grand Resonator wins.

The genius of the format: **bigger, more powerful ships need MORE energy**. A sailboat crew might only need a few minor nodes. A dreadnought crew needs dozens. This creates natural strategic tension between speed, firepower, and efficiency.

# Harmony's Integrated Nodes

Every territory in Harmony is anchored by a **node**---a pocket of concentrated reality that produces a unique elemental resource. When integrated, these nodes contribute to Harmony's [[The Power System|Global Amplitude]] and provide materials that shape civilization.

## Node Power Scale

| Rating | Description | Global Amplitude Impact | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Major** | Transforms the lives of large sentient populations; forms the backbone of civilization | +100 to +800 amps | Gilded (Chime Spires), Feltland (Live Soil), Juxta (Lift Stone), Octo (Speaking Stones) |
| **Moderate** | Significant resource with broad applications; supports multiple industries | +30 to +100 amps | Most integrated Harmony nodes |
| **Minor** | Useful but limited in scope; often a single unique property | +5 to +30 amps | Trail-Tail Trench, smaller frontier nodes |
| **Shattered (Major)** | The six ancient clan nodes---Major in size but currently outputting Minor power due to the Shattered Circuit effect | Varies (see anomaly) | Ash-Bloods, Mizizi, Trench-Kin, Renali, Wadi, Fulgur-Born |

The distinction between major and minor is not purely about raw power---it's about the degree to which the node has transformed sentient life around it.

---

## The Integrated Nodes of Harmony

### 1. Gilded (Major)
- **Resource:** Chime Spires (Resonators)
- **Faction:** [[house-gilded|House Gilded]] ([[House Gilded|The Highborne]])
- **Description:** Home city of [[house-gilded|House Gilded]]. The Chime Spires are musical, organ-like structures that synchronize raw ether into usable power. They are the foundation of Harmony's entire energy infrastructure.
- **Status:** Core Harmony territory. The original spires still function as the backbone of the power grid.

### 2. Umbra Mountain (Major)
- **Resource:** Umber Crystals
- **Faction:** House Vox (The Spark)
- **Description:** Home mountain of House Vox. Source of the umber crystals used in every generation of Vox crystal batteries, from the first post-Night-of-Sparks surge protectors to the current Panda line.
- **Status:** Core Harmony territory. Heavily mined and industrialized.

### 3. Feltland (Major)
- **Resource:** Live Soil
- **Description:** A vast plain near the seat of Harmony Prime. The soil is extraordinarily fertile---essentially supercharged fertilizer that accelerates growth far beyond natural rates.
- **Status:** Core Harmony territory. Agricultural backbone of the empire.

### 4. Octo (Major)
- **Resource:** Speaking Stones
- **Description:** One-way communication stones. The first stone transmits; the second stone receives and reproduces the message. This is how long-distance communication works across Harmony---essentially magical radio.
- **Status:** Core Harmony territory. Speaking Stone networks span the empire.

### 5. Juxta (Major)
- **Resource:** Lift Stone
- **Description:** When an object is fully encapsulated in Lift Stone, it weighs only half what it should. The stone itself is just barely weightless (net-zero weight). Essential for construction, shipping, and the mobile architecture of cities like Vumbua.
- **Status:** Core Harmony territory. Critical infrastructure material.

### 6. Shepta
- **Resource:** Whetstone
- **Description:** Liquid stone that hardens within seconds of being separated from its source pool. Essentially instant concrete. Pour it, shape it, and it sets almost immediately.
- **Status:** Integrated. Major construction resource.

### 7. Lenoa
- **Resource:** Friction Needles
- **Description:** Two needles struck together eliminate friction from the next friction interaction they encounter, until the needles break. Strike the pair, touch them to an ice skate, and the skate glides across ground as if on ice---the friction is absorbed into the needles instead of being applied.
- **Status:** Integrated. Used in transportation and industrial applications.

### 8. Relina
- **Resource:** Soft Forge
- **Description:** A lake in which anything submerged immediately loses its rigid shape and becomes malleable. While within the lake, objects can be reshaped freely. Extremely dangerous---works on organic matter too, so hands lose their shape if submerged. The city constantly innovates clever tools and techniques for safe use.
- **Status:** Integrated. Premier metallurgy and crafting center.

### 9. Minnow
- **Resource:** Living Scent Wood
- **Description:** A forest where, as long as a tree is still alive, anything that touches it permanently gains the scent of that wood. The effect is irreversible and lasts forever.
- **Status:** Integrated. Source of luxury goods, preservation, and ceremonial materials.

### 10. Bloomfield
- **Resource:** Clockwork Blooms
- **Faction:** [[Scrivener Guild]] ([[Scrivener Guild|The Loreborne]])
- **Description:** Flowers that bloom with mechanical precision every 60 seconds. For centuries, Clockwork Blooms were the standard unit of timekeeping across Harmony before more precise instruments were developed. The Scriveners adopted these as their timekeeping standard, essential for synchronizing maps, logs, and research across the empire.
- **Status:** Integrated. Historical significance; still used ceremonially.

### 11. Prima
- **Resource:** Dew Crystals
- **Description:** Crystals that condense water from ambient air, producing roughly a cup of clean water each. Invaluable in desert regions and aboard ships where fresh water is scarce.
- **Status:** Integrated. Essential survival resource for frontier operations.

### 12. Settika
- **Resource:** Prism Falls
- **Description:** Water that responds to conceptual imbuement (revealing aligned moments in a person's soul) or, in its base state, acts as a diagnostic tool that changes color to illuminate physical, structural, or spiritual disharmonies (like disease, cracked wood/bone, or physical disguises).
- **Status:** Integrated. Used in medicine, security, and engineering inspection.

### 13. Nstyl
- **Resource:** Silent Silt
- **Description:** When a handful or more of this silt is gathered in one spot, it absorbs all sound in the surrounding area. The city that grew around this node communicates entirely through sign language---spoken language is physically impossible there.
- **Naming Convention:** Nstyl names are **signed, not spoken.** Each person's name is a specific hand-sign---a gesture, not a word. The written forms (e.g. "Nstyl," "Zyykl," "Tus") are phonetic approximations created by outsiders attempting to transcribe the hand-shapes into letters. Different speakers produce wildly different pronunciations of the same name---"Zyykl" might become "Zekelef" or "Zickle" depending on who's trying to say it. Nstyl people consider this amusing, not offensive. Their real names exist only in gesture.
- **Status:** Integrated. Used in stealth operations, hospitals, and sound-sensitive environments.

### 14. Elysium
- **Resource:** Snow Sand
- **Description:** Desert sand that causes vivid hallucinations and illusions of whatever the affected person most desires. Highly dangerous and highly regulated.
- **Status:** Integrated. Strictly controlled; used in medicine and interrogation.

### 15. Bay of Breath
- **Resource:** Breathable Algae
- **Description:** An algae colony that produces and purifies breathable air. Essential for deep-hull operations, submarine expeditions, and enclosed environments.
- **Status:** Integrated. Critical life-support resource.

# The World of the Great Stitching

The world is not a static globe; it is a disjointed reality waiting to be pulled together.

## Core Concepts

### The Great Stitching

Nodes act as gravity wells for reality. When a new Node is claimed and harmonized, it physically pulls the new continent slowly toward the central Harmony continent. The world is literally being built/stitched together over time.

### The Threat (The Ether/Nether)

If civilization stops expanding, the reality of claimed lands begins to dissolve. This has happened before, creating the [[The Bleed|Dissolved Lands]].

### The Crisis

For centuries, Harmony had excess power. Now, for the first time, they are approaching [[The Minimum]]. If they dip below the threshold, Harmony tech will begin to lose charge.

## Integration Mechanics

Integration is the process of locking two disparate realities into a single flow. It requires a simultaneous, two-way energy exchange:

1. **Inward Flow:** Raw material of the new Node powers Harmony tech at 100% efficiency
2. **Outward Flow:** Harmony batteries power a local necessity in the new territory
3. **The Lock:** When both flows occur, realities fuse and a Safe Lane opens

See the [[Glossary]] for detailed term definitions.

4. **The Obsidian Ridges:** Volcanic glass and magma. Home of the [[Ash-Bloods]].
5. **The Abyssal Trench:** High-pressure underwater vents. Home of the [[Trench-Kin]].
6. **Tartannen:** The mythical "First City" of the old empire, rumored to be somewhere in this sector.

# The Power System

How Harmony keeps the lights on---and why they're flickering.

## What Players Know

Harmony runs on ether---a pervasive energy field that flows through integrated land. The more territory Harmony integrates, the stronger this field becomes. Crystal resonators power everything from streetlights to medical devices through tapping into that energy. The problem is that the energy ebbs and flows which is why teh night of sparks occurred and feeds the wealth and importance of the Vox family and their umbra crystals to act as resistors to ensure consistent power levels

### The Global Amplitude

The **Global Amplitude** is the total ether energy available across all of Harmony's integrated territory. It is not stored---it is a living, breathing field that rises and falls like a slow heartbeat.

**Key Properties:**
- Each time a new node is integrated into Harmony, the Global Amplitude increases
- Major nodes cause large jumps; minor nodes cause small bumps
- The amplitude has small natural surges and oscillations---it breathes like a living thing
- The Global Amplitude has been measured and documented for most of Harmony's recorded history

**Historical Values:**
- **~400 years ago:** ~350 amps (before Night of Sparks)
- **~400 years ago (post-surge):** ~450+ amps (after major node integration)
- **Era 4 (gradual rise):** Climbed to ~600 as minor nodes accumulated
- **Last 80 years (The Silent Era):** Plateaued at ~600, then began declining for the first time in recorded history
- **Today:** Resonance max ~600, but trending downward

### The Resonators (House Gilded)

The original power infrastructure, built by [[House Gilded]] during Era 1.

**How They Work:**
- **Chime Spires** are musical, almost organ-like structures mounted on buildings
- They synchronize with the ambient ether field, converting it into usable power
- Think of them as antennas that tune into a broadcast signal, not generators
- The ether power is always available---the resonators simply tap into it

**Limitation:**
- No surge protection. Whatever the Global Amplitude is, that's what flows through
- When a major node integrated ~400 years ago and the amplitude jumped 30%, every resonator in Harmony overloaded simultaneously
- This was the **Night of Sparks**---massive fires, infrastructure destruction, and the near-ruin of [[House Gilded]]

### The Vox Crystal Batteries (House Vox)

Developed after the Night of Sparks using **umber crystals** mined from **Umbra Mountain** (House Vox's home base).

**Important Misconception:** These are not batteries in the traditional sense. They do not store energy. They are more like **wireless chargers** or **regulated antennas** that control the connection to the ether field.

**Innovation #1: Surge Protection**
- The umber crystal matrix caps the amount of power accepted from the ether
- Think of it as a Wi-Fi signal limiter---the power is always available, but the crystal restricts how much flows through
- This prevents the overloading that caused the Night of Sparks
- Current Panda line batteries are set at **550 amps** (safely below the ~600 Global Amplitude max)

**Innovation #2: Stored Connection**
- The crystals can store the **connection to the land** inside their matrix
- Previously, you had to be within proximity of a node to get any power from resonators
- With a Vox crystal battery, the resonator is "convinced" it's still part of Harmony's land, even when it's not
- This is a stored **connection**, not stored **energy**---an important distinction most scientists get wrong

**The Panda Line (Current Generation):**
- Allows operation up to **3 days outside any node's range**
- Duration varies based on Global Amplitude and surrounding conditions (predicting exactly when it cuts out is an art)
- When the stored connection expires, power drops to **absolute zero**---no gradual decline
- About car-battery sized; each lasts ~1 year before the surge protection degrades and output becomes inconsistent
- Set at 550 amps

**The Apex 1 (Scrapped Next Generation):**
- Designed in anticipation of the Ash-Blood integration surge
- Projections estimated the Global Amplitude would jump from 600 to 900, 1100, or even 1400 (a major node should add +300 to +800)
- The Apex 1 was built with surge protection rated above those projected lines
- **Now scrapped:** The Ash-Blood integration only produced a ~20 amp increase, making the Apex 1's specifications irrelevant
- All marketing and rollout plans are moot

### The Ash-Blood Anomaly

This small amplitude change is the central mystery that [[professor-kante|Professor Kante]] is investigating (revealed in [[session-02.5|Session 2.5]]):

**Expected:** A major node integration (like the [[Ash-Bloods]]) should produce a massive Global Amplitude increase (+300 to +800 amps), similar to the surge that caused the Night of Sparks ~400 years ago.

**Actual:** The Ash-Blood integration produced only a ~20 amp increase---barely better than a minor node discovered 80 years ago.

**Implications:**
1. Why did a major node produce minor node results?
2. If the Global Amplitude drops below the Panda line's 550 amp setting, the batteries can no longer provide consistent output
3. If the amplitude drops further below storage thresholds, the ether field's natural breathing/surging will cause power output to become erratic and unpredictable
4. The Shattered Circuit theory may explain the leak---the Ash-Blood node is "reaching out" to its missing siblings, bleeding energy into the void

### The Great Surge (Endgame) - CURRENTLY UNKNOWN BY ANY PARTY IN HARMONY

The Shattered Circuit theory (which has not yet been established in Harmony) predicts that when ALL SIX ancient clans are fully integrated, the Global Amplitude will spike to **2,000+**---far beyond anything in recorded history. This would be catastrophic for current infrastructure:

- **Vox Crystal Batteries will shatter:** The Panda line is rated at 550 amps. Even the scrapped Apex 1 was only designed for 900-1,400. A 2,000+ surge would overwhelm every crystal battery in Harmony, repeating the Night of Sparks on a vastly larger scale.
- **The solution requires old and new:** Harmony would need the original Gilded "Flow" technology (Chime Spires that channel raw ether without caps) combined with [[Trench-Kin]] grounding to safely absorb and distribute the surge.
- **The paradox:** Harmony must integrate all six clans to survive, but doing so will destroy the very battery infrastructure keeping the lights on---unless they solve the surge problem first.

### The Ticking Clock

If the Global Amplitude continues to decline:
- **Above 550:** Business as usual (current Panda line works)
- **At 550:** Batteries begin inconsistent output as natural amplitude surges dip below the cap
- **Below 550:** Systematic power failures begin
- **Well below 550:** [[the-minimum|The Minimum]]---lights go out, medical devices fail, Harmony's infrastructure collapses

Rusted, non-magical metal gears, combustion engines, and plastic found in the trash vortex.

Useless junk from civilizations that failed to achieve Harmony. Protocol: Melt it down for raw materials.

Old mechanical parts occasionally turn up in scrap heaps. The Academy considers them worthless curiosities.

Remnants of the "First Weavers" who built the world without magic.

This tech doesn't require the Loom to function. **It is immune to "[[The Minimum]]."**

The edge of the map where the land turns into pixelated static and grey mist.

## Harmony View: "Dimensional Instability"

A lack of geometric scaffolding. The land is simply "falling off" the map. Harmony engineers treat it as a structural problem to be solved with anchors and walls.

The world is a dream. When the dreamer wakes up, the edges fade.

## The Arena: The Apex Ring

The Circuit-Run takes place in a massive natural geographic basin called the **Apex Ring**.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Size** | ~1 mile wide |
| **Terrain** | Static wilderness — canyons, petrified forests, dry sea-beds, open water |
| **Layout** | Consistent across all Harmony venues, but updated to reflect newly integrated territory |
| **Grand Resonator** | Towering obsidian pylon at the exact centre — the finish line |
| **Rim** | Operates like an airport — circular tram system with stops along the way |
| **Start Line** | At the basin's southern rim; rigs are dropped or slung from the amphitheaters |

### This Year's Special Layout

The arena layout is being **updated for the first time** to include the **Ash-Blood Isles** and the **Mizizi Forest island**. This is a huge deal — the entire population is seeing simulated versions of the newest territories for the first time.

> **GM Note:** Part of the Reso Race's purpose is to **teach Harmony's people about newly integrated land** — what it looks like, what resources it produces, what it brings to the Republic. The race is propaganda as much as sport.

### The Controversy: Missing Ash-Blood Node

The Spire locations are determined by [[scrivener-guild|Scrivener Guild]] calculations based on known node resonance values. **However, the Ash-Blood node data has not been released.** Despite the terrain reflecting the Ash-Blood Isles, there is no Ash-Blood Spire in the arena.

This is a point of public disappointment and quiet controversy. The Scriveners claim the numbers aren't finalised. The truth is more complicated — the [[The Power System|Ash-Blood Anomaly]] means the expected +300 to +800 amp surge only produced ~20 amps. Releasing those numbers would expose the failure.

---

## The Energy System: "Stored Connection"

The Circuit-Run's mechanics are built on the same technology as [[The Power System|Vox Crystal Batteries]], but used in a clever reverse application.

### How It Works

1. **Umber crystals** normally store a **connection to the land** — allowing devices to operate away from nodes by "convincing" the resonator it's still part of Harmony's territory (see [[The Power System#Innovation 2 Stored Connection]])
2. In a Circuit-Run, umber crystals are used **backwards** — they **sever** the craft's natural connection to the ambient ether field
3. This creates an artificial "dead zone" around the Rig — it operates at **zero power** until the crew reconnects
4. The crew must then **manually reopen** that connection by striking Spires and siphoning node energy
5. The amount of reconnection needed depends on the craft's power draw:

| Craft Type | Power Draw | Nodes Needed | Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Racing sailboat | Near-zero | A few minor nodes | Speed + agility, avoid conflict |
| Standard Venture-Rig | Medium | Several mixed nodes | Balanced approach |
| Heavy land-ship | High | Many nodes, including majors | Crew-heavy, can fight for contested nodes |
| Gryphon rider | Special | Massive (see note) | Only the heater is "powered"; the gryphon itself is free energy, but handicapped by an artificial umber gutter |

### The Win Condition

A team wins when their accumulated resonance **exceeds the Global Amplitude** plus their craft's rated power draw. At that point, they have enough "connection" to light the Grand Resonator.

- A low-power sailboat only needs to slightly exceed Global Amplitude
- A high-power dreadnought must generate connection equal to Global Amplitude **plus** its own massive energy consumption
- This is what makes the sport strategically interesting — raw power is a liability

> **Connection to [[professor-kante|Professor Kante]]'s Lessons:** The Circuit-Run is a practical demonstration of everything Kante teaches. The "stored connection" mechanic, the relationship between nodes and Global Amplitude, the difference between energy and connection — students who paid attention in class have a genuine advantage understanding the race.

## The Craft: Venture-Rigs

Teams or soloists bring their own mechanical crafts. There are no restrictions on size or crew count, but every craft must follow these rules:

### Mandatory Equipment

| Component | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Harvest-Prong** | A copper-and-crystal rod that physically strikes a Spire to siphon energy. Every rig must have one. |
| **Umber Gutter** | The reversed umber crystal that creates the artificial dead zone. Calibrated to the craft's rated power. |
| **HTech Power System** | The craft **must** be powered by Harmony technology in order to store the connection. No natural/organic power sources allowed (this is why gryphon riders are heavily handicapped). |

### Craft Scaling

Rigs have **no internal engines** in the traditional sense — they move only as long as they have an active "Node-Sync" in their battery. The initial drop from the rim gives momentum, but after that, everything depends on how quickly you can strike your first Spire.

## The Spires (Nodes)

During the **Loom-Pulse** at the start of each match, massive metallic pillars erupt from the ground in semi-randomised locations across the basin.

### Spire Rules

- **Randomised placement:** Locations are calculated by the [[scrivener-guild|Scrivener Guild]], but appear random to spectators
- **New spires emerge** as the race progresses — some appear mid-match
- **Limited charges:** Each Spire has a finite number of "syncs." Once depleted, it retracts into the ground, forcing latecomers to explore deeper
- **Major Nodes** are always the most sought-after — they provide the biggest connection boost but are heavily contested

### Syncing with a Spire

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| **1. Tune** | The pilot matches the Rig's frequency to the Spire's vibration |
| **2. Strike** | The Rig physically touches the Spire with the Harvest-Prong |
| **3. Consume** | The connection energy is absorbed into the Rig's umber gutter |

### Node Spectrum & Specific Spires

Each node type grants a temporary **Boon** — a power-up that lasts until the next sync. Spires are categorized by their Connection Units (CU).

#### 🏆 The Major Spires (The Game-Changers)

# 🏗️ Resonance Race: The Enterprise & Pit-Row

## 🏕️ The Apex Fringe (Pit-Row)

When the Resonance Race begins, the population of Vumbua effectively doubles. Since the Academy cannot (or will not) house 250,000 visitors, a massive, semi-permanent shanty-town erupts outside the Southern Rim. This is the **Apex Fringe**, more commonly known as **Pit-Row**.

- **The Vibe:** A sea of canvas tents, shipping containers, and open-air workshops. The air is thick with the smell of cheap grease, "Reso-Corn," and illegal aether-burning.
- **The Economy:** It is a pure barter-and-coin economy. While the Academy uses Loom-credits, Pit-Row runs on **Umber Shards**, copper, and favors.
- **The Crowd:** A mix of "Rig-Groupies" (obsessive fans who follow teams), disgraced former pilots, freelance mechanics looking for a contract, and bookies.

---

## 🎲 The Betting Syndicate (The Umber-Bet)

The official Academy stance on gambling is "Strictly Prohibited." In Pit-Row, it is the primary pastime.

- **The Iron Union Influence:** The betting rings are managed by the **Iron Union** (the same guild running the black market). They use specialized **"Bet-Cubes"**—mechanical devices that lock in a wager and only release the payout when they receive a specific "Victory Pulse" from the Grand Resonator.
- **The Odds:** Odds fluctuate wildly based on Monday/Tuesday hangar reports. If a team blows an engine in prep, the Syndicate knows within ten minutes.
- **High-Stakes Wagers:** Wealthy sponsors from [[House Gilded]] or [[House Vox]] often send proxies to Pit-Row to place massive bets on their own teams, sometimes as a form of "insurance" against a loss.

---

## 🔒 The "Impossible" Security (Why you can't just steal a rig)

The Academy knows that the rigs in the hangars represent millions of copper in technology and the future of the Ventures. They don't just use locks; they use **Resonant Anchoring**.

### 1. The Soul-Sync (Resonance Varnish)
Every sanctioned rig is calibrated to the **Resonant Frequency** of its registered pilot. The controls are coated in a thin, invisible layer of **Resonance Varnish**.
- **The Barrier:** If anyone else touches the controls, the varnish triggers an "Aether-Lock," flooding the cockpit with non-lethal but incapacitating static.
- **The Key (The Prism Flush):** To steal a ship, you need a vial of **Settika Prism Water**. Because it reveals "true nature," a **Prism Flush** can temporarily "strip" the varnish or reveal the exact biometric frequency needed for an Umber-battery to mimic the pilot's sync.

### 2. The Tethered Grid
While in the hangars, rigs are physically and energetically tethered to the **Academy Anchor-Main**.
- **The Barrier:** The rigs have zero internal power. They draw "Lease-Energy" through a massive copper umbilical. If the umbilical is disconnected without a "Clearance Pulse" from the Hangar-Boss, the rig's steering locks and it becomes a 10-ton paperweight.
- **The Bypassing:** You either need to hack the Hangar-Boss's terminal or use a **"Prism-Doped" battery**—an experimental umber cell that uses Prism Water to "trick" the tether into thinking the rig is still connected.

# Resonance Race: Pre-Race Preparations & Hangar Culture

*When the PCs inevitably blow off studying to go tinkering, this is what they find.*

## The Venue: The Partitioned Hangars
The massive Great Hangars—where the harrowing entrance exams were held—have been entirely transformed. The cavernous space is now divided by heavy canvas tarps, blast-glass barricades, and temporary sound-dampening fields into dozens of individual **Team Bays**. 

*   **The Atmosphere:** The air is thick with the smell of ozone, aether-oil, and desperation. The constant whine of power-drills is underlaid by the deeper, chest-rattling hum of Resonance Engines being redlined. Sleep-deprived students are practically living in their bays, sleeping on cots under their rig chassis.
*   **Academy Security:** The entrances to the hangar and the individual aisles are heavily monitored by the Academy's elite **Turbaned Guards**. They are stern, incorruptible, and armed with stun-pikes. 
*   **The Access Rule:** You cannot just walk into the hangars. Access requires a physical ledger sign-in, and to enter a specific team's bay, you must have explicit, real-time permission from a **sober team member** of that bay. The guards rigorously enforce the "sober" ruling and perform a daily **"Settika Sweep"**—using Prism Water to reveal any illegal modifications or unauthorized aether-leaks.

## The Fringe Connection
While the Academy controls the hangars, the "world outside" is where the real business happens. For more on the shadow economy, betting, and the area outside the walls, see **[[reso-race-enterprise]]**.

---

## Approaching the Hangars: The Crowd Filter
The path from the Academy proper to the Hangars on Tuesday night is a sea of 50,000 people. It’s not just a walk; it’s a gauntlet of fans, journalists, pit-crews, and "Reso-Corn" vendors.

### The Mechanic: The Crowd Crawl
If the PCs want to visit the hangars on Tuesday/Wednesday, they must navigate the chaos. This costs **Time**, the one resource they are currently short on.
- **The Roll:** **2d10 x 10 minutes.** (Avg: 110 mins).
- **The Modifiers:**
  - **High Check (DC 15 Social/Tech):** They find a shortcut or "fast-lane." Halve the time cost.
  - **Critical Fail:** They get caught in a "Team Riot" or a betting brawl. Double the time cost.

### Getting Past the Gate: Three Ways "In"
The Turbaned Guards at the main gates are strictly enforcing the "Pilot/Crew Only" rule. The PCs need an "In":

1.  **The Vendor Proxy:** A stressed "Reso-Corn" vendor’s cart has a broken axle near the gate. If the PCs help fix it (Tech check) or help push it in, the vendor (Ollie) will claim they are his "Supply Assistants."

## The Night Shift: Groundskeeping & Cleaning
While the pilots sleep (or pretend to), the **Sweepers** emerge. These are the people who keep the Academy from drowning in "Reso-Corn" wrappers and leaked aether-oil. They see everything.

### Key NPCs:
*   **"Old Mudge":** A cynical, hunched-over janitor with a long-handled brush and a perpetual scowl.
    *   **The Hook:** Mudge has seen every race for 30 years. He knows where the "blind spots" are in the hangar security cameras because that's where people stash their illegal trash. For a pack of "Mist-Cured Tobacco," he'll tell the PCs which bays are currently unmonitored.
*   **"Squeak":** A twitchy, teenage scavenger working the "Aether-Gutter" detail. 
    *   **The Hook:** Squeak isn't just cleaning; he's looking for discarded tech-scrap. If **[[iggy|Iggy]]** has been throwing away "junk" parts, Squeak might have found them. He's desperate to build his own rig one day and is a fountain of gossip about which teams are fighting behind the tarps.
*   **"Trawl":** A massive, silent individual who handles the heavy-duty **Settika Sweeps** at 3 AM.
    *   **The Hook:** Trawl carries a high-pressure **Prism Water sprayer** to melt away aetheric residue. He’s the most likely source for the PCs to "borrow" a vial of Prism Water if they can catch him during a smoke break or distract him while he's cleaning the Basin's lower drains.
2.  **The Technical Bluff:** The PCs dress in oily overalls and carry a crate of "Resonance Varnish." A successful **Deception** or **Tech (Persuasion)** check can convince the guard they are the "Night-Shift Calibration Team" for a minor squad (like the *Silver-Silt*).
3.  **The VIP Tailgate:** **Valentine Sterling** or a House Vox Scion is entering with a massive entourage of 20 sycophants. If the PCs can blend into the back of the group and look "high-society" enough, the guards won't bother checking every face.
4.  **The Krell Connection:** If they have a favor with **[[sergeant-krell|Sergeant Krell]]**, he might be on gate duty and simply nod them through with a stern "Don't make me regret this" look.

## The Ultimate Prize: "Venture Licensing"
While pride, house standing, and grades are on the line, the true, unspoken obsession driving the students to madness is the **Fast-Track License**.

*   **The Reward:** If a craft performs exceptionally well—winning the race outright, or simply being rated by the Council of Venturers as "Mist-Worthy"—the team can be immediately granted status as a **Licensed Crew for the Ventures**.
*   **What it Means:** They bypass the rest of the grueling Academy curriculum. They receive direct Vumbua funding, immediate access to exploration charters, and are exempt from the standard requirement of taking on "candidates" (freshmen burdens). 
*   **The Catch:** It is virtually impossible for Second-Years to achieve this without the backing of a powerful sponsor (like House Gilded or House Vox) or a seasoned adventurer pledging to ride with them post-race. 
*   **The Dark History:** It *has* happened to Second-Years in the past. It usually ends in tragedy. Unseasoned crews with fast-track licenses often push too deep on their first real Venture and never return, their high-performance racing rigs completely unsuited for the endurance required in the deep Mist.

## Engineering Philosophy: Race vs. Reality
Because the ultimate prize is real-world licensing, teams are torn between two engineering philosophies:
1.  **The Sprinters:** Teams building hyper-light, dangerous rigs designed purely to win the race. They strip armor and life-support for speed, betting it all on crossing the finish line first to secure a rich sponsor. 
2.  **The Realists:** Teams (often the poorer or un-sponsored ones) who are using this as an excuse to build their actual, real-life Venture craft. Their rigs might be slower in the race, but they are durable, heavily armored, and built to survive the actual monsters in the Mist. They hope the Council judges them on *survival*, not just speed.

### Scene: Finding Rill

**The Setup:** The PCs need to find **[[rill|Rill]]** — she invited **[[britt|Britt]]** and **[[aggie|Aggie]]** to visit her at the grass-covered building near [[block-99|Block 99]] back in Session 2. But when they arrive, she's not there. She's busy prepping for the opening Resonance Race.

**Finding her requires effort:**

- Her usual spot (the grass-covered building inset into the hill) is **empty**. Bark samples are scattered on the desk, half-finished notes, a cold cup of something fungal.
- If PCs ask around: *"Rill? She's been running between the Basin and the Fungal Labs all morning. Something about calibrations."*
- If PCs check the Fungal Labs: She's there with **[[professor-ink|Professor Ink]]** — the [[scrivener-guild|Scrivener Guild]] Captain and fungal biology specialist. They're hunched over what looks like living moss samples, arguing about "resonance frequencies in petrified bark."

**The Encounter:**

When the PCs finally track her down, Rill is distracted, stressed, and clearly conflicted. She's happy to see [[Britt]] and [[Aggie]] — but there's guilt behind the smile.

**What Rill reveals (if pushed):**

1. **The Journey:** She and **[[professor-ink|Professor Ink]]** are planning an expedition **back into the [[mizizi-petrified-forest|Mizizi Petrified Forest]]**. Ink wants to study the trees — specifically what they *remember*. Rill wants to find a cure for the "Eternal Growth" corruption affecting the [[mizizi|Mizizi]].
2. **Her Shame:** Rill left the Forest two years ago. The [[Mizizi]] see her as a **traitor**. She didn't just leave — she was the first to walk out. To go *back*, escorted by a Harmony academic... that's going to feel like an invasion to the Elders.
  > *"I know what they'll say. 'She left, and now she brings outsiders.' But Ink can see things in the bark that I can't. The trees are dying, [[britt|Britt]]. Slowly. And the Elders won't let anyone help because they think stagnation IS health."*
3. **Her Secret (GM discretion):** Rill is secretly [[Wadi]], not [[Mizizi]]. Her River Clan heritage gives her a different perspective on "Flow" vs "Stagnation" — it's why she can solve problems true Mizizi cannot. She won't reveal this unless deeply trusted.
4. **The Danger:** The heart of the [[Mizizi Petrified Forest]] is **protected**. The deeper you go, the more dangerous it gets:
  - **Hallucinations** — the forest's defence mechanism. The petrified trees project visions that confuse intruders
  - **Beasts** — creatures adapted to live in the hallucinogenic environment, potentially immune to the confusion
  - **The Elders** — the Mizizi leadership doesn't welcome visitors. They may view Rill's return as a threat

**Emotional Stakes:**

- For **[[britt|Britt]]**/**[[aggie|Aggie]]**: Rill is the only other Mizizi they know at the Academy. She's asking them to understand why she left — and maybe to support her return.
- For **other PCs**: This is a window into Mizizi culture, the Petrified Forest's dangers, and a future quest hook.
- For **Rill**: She's torn between her excitement about the journey and her terror of what she'll face when she gets there. She's also trying to manage Zephyr, prep the Resonance Race, and assist the Dean — she's stretched thin.

**Professor Ink — Quick Reference:**

| **Specialty**            | Fungal biology, petrified bark analysis                                                                                                               |

| **Relationship to Rill** | Research partners. He provides the scientific rigour; she provides the cultural context and access.                                                   |


**GM Notes:**

- This scene should feel **quiet and personal** — a contrast to the party and the ambush. It's a conversation, not an encounter.
- Don't force Rill to reveal everything. Let the PCs earn her trust through roleplay.
- If a PC offers to help with the forest expedition, that's a **major future quest hook** — flag it.
- If [[Iggy]] is present, Professor Ink will light up. There's a natural bond there.
