## CHAPTER 116: EIGHT VIALS

Rill knelt beside the heavy pack, carefully retrieving a cluster of eight small glass vials filled with dark, shimmering liquid. "I have enough for eight of us," she said, looking up across the clearing at the towering redwood-scale trunk of the ancient oak. "Again, this won't cure it. If we walk into this, we'll eventually have to find my homeland in the canyon to cure us. But it will buy time."

Aggie walked past the perimeter, pausing beside one of the outer trees without stepping into the circle of violet spores. She pressed her fingertips against the rough wood, listening for any hum of life, but the outer timber remained quiet and cold as stone.

Iggy didn't wait to be invited.

The little clay creature snatched a vial from Rill's open palm, uncorked it with his teeth, and chugged the entire dose in one violent swallow. Without a second thought, he rolled straight forward—scrambling right through the glowing ring of fungal spores and charging directly at the colossal tree. The ancient oak rose like a redwood titan before him, vast enough to shelter an entire army beneath its roots. Finding a deep, moss-lined furrow in the petrified bark, Iggy wedged his clay form into the crevice, closed his water-filled goggles, and slumped instantly into a deep, motionless slumber against the living wood. <!-- L1601 -->

Ignatius blinked, looking around the clearing in confusion. "Where'd Iggy go? Wait—did he just fall asleep immediately?"

Before anyone could answer, Zephyr snatched a vial of her own, vaulted over the roots, and charged headlong through the fungal mist to slap her palm flat against the petrified trunk, dropping into the moss beside Iggy a heartbeat later.

Ignatius threw his hands in the air, embers flaring wildly at his cuffs. "Fuck, I better go," he groaned. "Iggy is going to be so pissed that Zephyr followed him in there!"

Britt stepped forward, rolling her shoulders and staring down at the vials in Rill's hands. Her dreadlocks of living root shifted with quiet resolve. "I'm going too," she said firmly. "I already carry the infection anyway, so I might as well." <!-- L1608 -->

Across the clearing, Pip hopped around Saffron, peering up at the quiet Renali scholar. "Saffron, why are you shaking your head?" Pip demanded, bouncing on her heels. "Are you a clan member? Saffron? Saffron!" <!-- L1627 -->

"Pip, let her be," Bramble rumbled softly, extending a vine to gently tug the excitable pixie back.

Professor Ink adjusted her brass spectacles, stepping forward to study the glowing veins pulsing beneath the bark. "I believe one representative from each of the clans should take a vial," the professor observed. "And perhaps two others—those who feel the deepest resonance with the network—should join them."

Rill nodded in quiet agreement. "Wherever this leads, we need to go together. It shouldn't be me—I don't possess a connection to the Mizizi network. But two non-clansmen who are willing to take the risk could step forward."

Loami pushed his flat cap back, stepping out from the shadow of the stone terrace. "I'll take it," he said simply, "if you're willing." <!-- L1628 -->

"Makes sense to me, Loami," Rill replied, extending a vial toward him.

Loami took the glass cylinder, popped the stopper, and downed the bitter draught in a single gulp. He grimaced, fished a greasy rag from his pocket to wipe his mouth, and chased the taste with a familiar swipe of soot and engine grease before walking calmly to the tree and placing his bare hand against the bark. His knees folded beneath him as the ancient resonance pulled him under. <!-- L1632 -->

Saffron took a slow, steadying breath. Firming her posture, the silent scholar accepted her vial, drank, and stepped up to lay her slender fingers against the living wood, collapsing gently into the ferns.

That left only a single open place.

Lucky and Pip stepped forward at the exact same instant, stopping inches apart and glaring at each other.

Lucky crossed his arms, his confident smile wavering slightly as he eyed the ominous, pulsing spores. "Look... it just kind of feels like if one of us is going to get through this without a scratch, it's probably going to be me." <!-- L1643 -->

Pip planted her tiny boots into the moss and jabbed a fierce finger at her own chest. "No, dude! They like me way more than you!"

Lucky hesitated, looking between the colossal petrified tree and the stubborn pixie. He gave a small, defeated shrug and took a step back, sweeping an arm toward the oak. "You know what? You're right. Be my guest."

Pip puffed her chest in triumph—and then froze.

Her giant eyes went wide with sudden, horrified realization. "Oh shit..." she squeaked, her voice climbing an octave. "Did I just volunteer myself?!" <!-- L1645 -->

She whipped around toward Rill, waving both hands in desperate agitation. "Give me! Give me!"

Snatching the final vial, Pip downed the liquid, sprinted full speed through the glowing spore cloud, and slammed her forehead with a resounding *thwack* straight into the petrified trunk—knocking herself out cold as she tumbled into a heap on the moss.
