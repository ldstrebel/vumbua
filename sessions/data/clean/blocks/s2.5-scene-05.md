## CHAPTER 20: INFILTRATING L-POWER

Iggy moved with the natural, unhurried stealth of creature carved from the earth itself. His feet made no sound against the flagstones; the packed-soil pads of his boots absorbed every vibration. Wrapped in his oversized traveling coat, he looked less like an intruder and more like a bundle of canvas discarded against the wall. <!-- L0401 --> <!-- L0404 --> <!-- L0406 -->

Back home in the Trench, sneaking around had never truly felt like sneaking. He had never been caught, not because he was a master thief, but because he was profoundly, thoroughly ignored. His parents had possessed clearance to every council chamber and maintenance conduit, and no one had ever bothered to question the quiet, dirt-stained youth who sat in the corner sketching pipe layouts. He wasn't clumsy. His people were the soil; rock gave way before him like yielding bread. Whenever a passage grew narrow, he simply slipped through the seam, as though the stone itself parted to let him pass. <!-- L0409 --> <!-- L0420 --> <!-- L0431 -->

Now, standing before the bronze portals of L-Power, he paused to peer through the thick, wire-reinforced glass panel. Inside, the grand receiving vestibule was submerged in amber twilight. Most of the gas lamps were doused, but behind a curved mahogany reception counter near the far wall, a pool of lamplight illuminated a curious sight: the tall, conical peak of a bright crimson felt hat. The hat was bobbing rhythmically from side to side, accompanied by the faint scratching of a steel-nibbed dip pen on heavy parchment. <!-- L0450 --> <!-- L0473 --> <!-- L0498 --> <!-- L0501 -->

A normal student might have hesitated, searching for authorization papers, an entrance slate, or a mentor's pass. But Iggy had never inhabited a world where doors were closed to curiosity. He did not knock. He did not formulate an alibi. He simply placed two earthy palms against the brass latch, pushed downward, and stepped inside. <!-- L0505 --> <!-- L0510 --> <!-- L0512 --> <!-- L0525 -->
