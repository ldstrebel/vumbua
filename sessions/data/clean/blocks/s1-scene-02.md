## THE FLAG THAT WOULDN'T RISE

The intake warehouse was an ocean of plywood cubicles stretched beneath zinc-sheet rafters, smelling of machine oil, ozone, wet parchment, and the cold, sour sweat of ten thousand anxious candidates. Every few seconds, the clatter of typewriters was punctuated by a sharp, tinny *ding* as another spring-loaded brass flag popped into the air, signaling completion. Almost instantly, an attendant in a fluorescent safety sash would materialize from the central aisle to sweep the candidate out of their seat and into the processing current. <!-- L0316 -->

Britt finished her survey first.

Her cubicle was barely wider than her shell — a laminate desk, an arcanatech terminal humming with coiled galvanic charge, and the slender brass stem of the completion flag mounted to the corner. She looked over the low divider. Two cubicles away, Aggie was still bent double over her desk, stylus flying across the paper in furious concentration, completely oblivious to the room around her. If Britt stood up now, the human conveyor belt would sweep her out into the crowd alone. Britt wasn't leaving her behind. <!-- L0317 -->

The terminal chimed. The brass mechanism clicked, preparing to fire.

Britt reacted instantly. She reached up and clamped her scaled palm over the top of the flag just as the spring tripped. The brass stem shuddered violently against her grip, buzzing and straining to lock itself upright, but she held it down with stubborn force. <!-- L0322 -->

"Hold on, girl," she whispered to herself.

Through the narrow slit between the cubicle walls, she could see the wide thoroughfare. A floor proctor in a bright orange sash was pacing the row, clipboard tucked under one arm. He paused mid-stride, his eyes narrowing as he scanned the cluster of desks — exactly like an airport traffic guard zeroing in on an idling carriage in a loading zone. He tapped his pencil against his board, spotted Britt's hunched silhouette, and began marching directly toward her booth, ready to blow the whistle. <!-- L0323 --> <!-- L0335 -->

Britt glanced back at Aggie. Still scribbling. She had maybe ten seconds before the proctor reached her. Merely holding the flag wouldn't work much longer; she had to manufacture a delay.

Thinking fast, Britt reached down to the slot on the terminal, seized the printed assessment ticket, and ripped it free with a sharp *snip*. She crumpled the paper into a tight ball, shoved it deep into the pocket of her traveler's tunic, and let the brass flag go. It sprang upright with a belated, triumphant *ding*. <!-- L0344 -->

The attendant arrived out of breath, hovering over the entrance to the cubicle. "Ma'am. You finished your exam." <!-- L0350 -->

Britt looked up, wide-eyed and thoroughly bewildered. "I did," she said, "but it didn't print." <!-- L0351 -->

The proctor blinked, gesturing impatiently toward the aisle. "Um. Okay. This way, please—" <!-- L0352 -->

"Impossible, ma'am," the proctor added, his brow furrowing in bureaucratic distress. "The mechanism is automatic. It prints upon completion." <!-- L0354 -->

"No, it didn't print. It didn't print!" Britt was already moving, sliding out of her chair onto the floor. "Let me — I'm going to look under the table." <!-- L0355 -->

Britt dropped onto all fours, crawling beneath the narrow plywood desk. She began shuffling through the sawdust and kicking at the floorboards, muttering loudly about missing slips and broken gears. <!-- L0357 -->

The attendant hovered outside the booth, his hands fluttering in rising panic. "Excuse me, ma'am! It really is time to go. This cubicle needs to be cleared for the next candidate. Excuse me, ma'am!" <!-- L0362 -->

"It didn't print!" Britt's muffled voice echoed from beneath the desk.

Deciding the scene had reached its peak, Britt braced her knees to stand. But she misjudged the clearance. Her massive tortoiseshell rose like a battering ram into the underside of the workstation. The entire desk bucked violently. The heavy arcanatech typewriter on top pitched forward, lost its footing on the slick laminate, and slid off the edge.

It hit the aisle floor with a thunderous *CRASH* — brass bells ringing, keys shearing off against the timber planks, internal counterweights rolling free, and an ink ribbon unraveling across the walkway like a spool of dark silk. The deafening racket silenced every cubicle within thirty paces.

In the dead, horrified quiet that followed, a single small sound cut through the air from the neighboring booth:

*DING!* <!-- L0363 -->

Aggie's completion flag sprang proudly into the air. Through the partition, Aggie looked up from her completed survey, blinking in quiet wonder, a faint, immaculate smile playing on her lips — timed with absolute, cinematic perfection. <!-- L0364 -->

In the aisle, Britt casually brushed the sawdust from her knees, slipped two fingers into her pocket, and produced the crumpled slip of parchment.

"Oh," she said, smiling cheerfully at the trembling attendant. "I found it."

The attendant stared between the shattered remnants of the typewriter and Britt's beaming face, his voice rising an octave in sheer desperation. "Please. Please follow me, ma'am. Please follow—" He turned and practically bolted down the aisle. <!-- L0371 -->

Britt caught Aggie's eye across the row — *let's go, right now* — and slipped into the walkway behind him. <!-- L0374 -->

Before they had gone ten yards, a second attendant came sprinting out of the corridor crush, clutching a fresh sheet of parchment and waving it wildly over her head. "Ma'am! Ma'am, excuse me, ma'am!" She skidded to a halt, thrust the replacement paper into Britt's hands, gasped a breathless "you're welcome," and was immediately swallowed back into the churning sea of applicants. There were hundreds of these exhausted proctors running through the aisles, shepherds frantically trying to manage thousands of candidates at once. <!-- L0375 -->

At the end of the aisle, the party converged again, drawn into the central concourse like tributaries joining a slow, swelling river. <!-- L0378 -->

Lomi fell in beside one of the proctors, his hands casually clasped behind his back, strolling with the easy confidence of a senior mechanic doing an inspection tour of a boiler works.

"There's quite a lot of you here," Lomi remarked, nodding toward the endless rows of booths. "How's — you know. How's your benefits? Everybody doing well? They paying you well here?" <!-- L0379 -->

The attendant cast a weary, bloodshot eye over Lomi's grease-stained woolen cap and canvas collar. "You are a copper," he said, flatly diagnosing the man's trade. "It is always good to know your options. I hope you make it through the next round. Um. Do you need a job? Are you looking for employment?" <!-- L0380 -->

Lomi chuckled, shaking his head. "Ah, no. I've got a life calling in the boiler room, you know? Family born and raised. Boilermaker union, going way back. And you know, this might take off." <!-- L0384 -->

The attendant opened his mouth to reply, then froze as raised voices cut through the drone of the hall. "Sorry. One second—" he said, darting away into the crowd. <!-- L0385 -->

A few paces ahead, a major confrontation had stalled the queue. A hulking proctor in dark utility canvas was wrestling with a rejected applicant, physically dragging him out of the thoroughfare.

"Come on, sir. Your test is done," the burly guard grunted, wrenching the man's grip from a support timber. "Please, please don't try it again. No, you can't. I'm sorry, it's — you just have to leave." <!-- L0387 -->

The rejected man was openly weeping, clinging desperately to the doorway. "Please! A second try, please! I need this for my family!"

A scuffle broke out down the line. Someone's leather cap went spinning over the cubicle dividers. The little proctor ducked swiftly as the hat whistled past his head, then hurried back to Lomi's side, straightening his crumpled coat.

"Oh, sorry about that," the proctor breathed, trying to reassemble his dignity. "Um. You were talking about boiling water."

"Yeah, you know, you are doing great work here, keep doing your thing," Lomi said warmly, patting the man's arm. "I see you're very busy. Just want to make sure you're being taken care of." <!-- L0394 -->

The proctor exhaled a long, hollow breath, looking up at Lomi with haunted eyes. "Gosh... thank you for your concern. I — if you have a god, please pray." He nodded grimly toward an eight-foot-tall Goliath candidate who had collapsed against the wall, massive shoulders shaking with devastating sobs as guards moved in to surround him. "No, no, a second try, please!" the Goliath begged into his hands. "I need this for my family!"

The little proctor watched the tragedy unfold with the numb, mechanical fatigue of someone who had watched it happen a thousand times that morning. "Yeah. So sad." <!-- L0395 -->

Ignatius, walking just behind them, watched the massive giant being escorted toward the side exits in tears. His brow furrowed, the glowing flames of his hair dimming to an uneasy crimson ember. "Why'd you make it so sad?" he muttered under his breath. <!-- L0401 -->

"I know," the proctor whispered back helplessly.

As the corridor carried them forward, the ruthless logic of the intake gauntlet became clear. About one in every five candidates was quietly diverted into side passages — their brass flags hadn't tripped correctly, or their mechanical totals had fallen short. There were no flashing red or green lights, no dramatic alarms; just a silent, systematic culling.

Nobody checked a single receipt along the concourse. Candidates clutched their printed slips like talismans, but the walkway attendants ignored them completely until the far end of the hall. There, the corridor narrowed into a fortified bottleneck manned by two broad, soldier-built guards in heavily wrapped jerkins over padded leather. They stood like warehouse gatekeepers with grease pencils, demanding each candidate's slip, checking the markings, and slashing a heavy black line across the parchment before permitting them to step through the iron gates. <!-- L0411 -->

Iggy, meanwhile, had stopped walking entirely.

He stood in the dead center of the corridor, heedless of the candidates bumping into his clay shoulders, staring upward with wide, round goggles at a bulky brass ticket-printer suspended from an overhead gantry. The machine was chittering furiously, extruding narrow paper strips in an unbroken rhythm like a mechanical tongue. <!-- L0416 -->

"I could use a tissue," Iggy announced to the room at large. <!-- L0418 -->

A passing attendant halted, staring down at the little soil-kin in bewilderment. "A… tissue?" <!-- L0420 -->

"That's okay. That's okay," Iggy chirped pleasantly. "I have an idea." <!-- L0427 -->

With the serene, unclouded confidence of someone who had never been taught that machines were fragile, Iggy walked up beneath the gantry, reached up both hands, seized the hanging paper ribbon, and threw his entire body weight downward. <!-- L0429 -->

*CRUNCH.*

The mounting bracket sheared straight off the beam. The entire brass assembly tore free with a terrifying shriek of stripped bolts, plummeting from the ceiling and smashing across the floor in an explosion of clattering gears, severed springs, and a geyser of thick, purple alchemical ink that sounded like a pipe organ falling down a flight of stone stairs. <!-- L0430 -->

Iggy stood amid the wreckage and the puddle of goo, beaming proudly as he waved a single liberated slip of paper in the air. "I got it! I got it, I got it!" <!-- L0434 -->

The attendant clutched his hair, his face turning an ashen gray. "Oh, god. That is — that just dropped our throughput by a hundred candidates! This is terrible! Terri—" <!-- L0435 -->

Iggy looked up through his ink-spattered goggles, completely untroubled. "I have one more question," he said. "Where am I?" <!-- L0436 -->

A taller figure in an immaculate charcoal uniform came shouldering through the crowd, eyes wide with horror as he took in the carnage. "What happened? Can you help me fix it?" The supervisor leaned down, poked a gloved finger at the oozing gears, and immediately jerked back in disgust. "No, it's already — oh, it's so gross!"

He spun on Iggy, fighting to keep his professional composure. "What — where you're in the test! Go that way! Just please stay with the—" <!-- L0441 --> <!-- L0443 -->

"But I don't know what this is," Iggy said politely, holding up the soggy scrap. <!-- L0444 -->

Before the supervisor could lose his temper, a second attendant — far more crisply tailored and moving with glacial administrative dignity — stepped smoothly into the breach, taking Iggy gently by the elbow. "Sir. This way. Towards the entrance. Welcome to Vumbua." <!-- L0453 -->

"Well, thank you so much," Iggy said warmly. "I do have questions." <!-- L0454 -->

"Absolutely no problem," the tailored proctor replied without a flicker of emotion. "If there are any questions — don't ask me. Keep them in, and they'll be answered." He offered a practiced half-bow. "Welcome to Curiosity." <!-- L0455 --> <!-- L0457 -->

"Good," Iggy decided, nodding with profound satisfaction. "Good. That's what you're here for." <!-- L0460 -->

At the end of the aisle, the receipt gauntlet awaited them. Iggy presented his torn, ink-dripping slip to the burly checkpoint guard. The guard looked at the blackened scrap, looked down at Iggy's round copper goggles and earnest clay face, then glanced back at the supervisor — who gave a slow, defeated shrug.

The guard let out a rough bark of laughter, shook his head, and clapped the little clay figure on the back hard enough to rattle his moss. "All right. Good job, lad." <!-- L0462 -->

"Perfect," Iggy said, and trotted through the gates. <!-- L0463 -->

When Britt and Aggie stepped up to the line, the atmosphere shifted noticeably. The two hardened guards looked up, their postures relaxing as they made deliberate eye contact.

"Oh — welcome," the first guard said, his voice surprisingly warm. "Welcome to Vumbua. Hope you have a great time, learn a lot, and may adventure take you where your heart desires. Good luck." <!-- L0464 -->

Britt blinked, offering a polite nod, and quickly pulled Aggie through into the open archway before anyone could change their mind. Behind them, thousands of other candidates were being waved through without a single word of greeting — leaving the quiet warmth of the guards' blessing hanging behind them like an intriguing mystery. <!-- L0466 -->
