## CHAPTER 110: THE LAB BELOW

Inside the humming hull of the four-legged walker *Elijah*, the machine settled into a rhythmic, lurching gallop across the savanna grasslands.

The main operations compartment opened into a spacious central lounge arranged around a long tactical table. Projecting from the center was an acoustic holographic display showing the vast terrain stretching between Vumbua and the distant Mizizi forest, with a plotted route cutting across the flat plains.

While the squad traded quick pleasantries and claimed bunks, Loami ambled toward the iron spiral staircase leading up to the open-air observation bridge, eager to take in his very first sight of the world beyond the city walls.

Up in the pilot's cradle at the forward helm sat a small, sharp-featured goblin driver, his clawed hands flicking brass toggle switches with bored, practiced efficiency. Standing beside the navigation console was Professor Ink, her sharp eyes studying the distant horizon.

Loami leaned over the brass railing with an easy mechanic's slouch. "Hey... so how hard is a beauty like this to drive?" <!-- L1050 -->

The goblin driver didn't even look over his shoulder. Reaching out with a clawed thumb, he slammed a red lever on the console that locked the mechanical steering on a dead-straight heading. "Hard enough that I don't... I mean, who's asking?" the goblin grunted flatly.

"Doesn't seem very hard," Loami grinned. "Anyway, name's Loami. Harbor mechanic. We're a crew scooped up to help out. First time out of Vumbua, so I just thought I'd come up and take in the surroundings. It's beautiful out here."

The goblin rolled his eyes, turning back to his dials. "Buzz off, man."

Loami laughed softly, leaning against the rail to watch the green savanna unspool beneath *Elijah*'s galloping stride.

Down in the operations lounge below, Rill stepped over to where Zephyr was lounging across a cushioned bench near Ignatius, offering a stiff, reserved nod. "I'm glad you're alive," Rill muttered politely.

Then Rill's gaze shifted to Britt, and the color instantly drained from her face. She gasped, slapping both hands against her cheeks.

"Oh gods... I completely forgot!" Rill exclaimed in sudden panic. She lunged forward, grabbing Britt's forearm with urgent force. "Britt, I need you down in the lower laboratory right now! This very second—fast as you can move!"

Without waiting for a response, Rill sprinted across the deck, threw back the heavy floor hatch, and slid down the ladder rungs like water down a conduit pipe.

"What's going on?" Britt asked, scrambling to follow.

Zephyr stretched out on the cushioned bench, crossing her boots and pulling her collar up. "No chance," Zephyr scoffed, shutting her eyes. "Labs are the absolute worst place to go. You go down there and it's seventeen days before you ever see daylight again."

Iggy hovered by the open hatch, his oversized copper goggles fixed in a narrow, suspicious squint upon Zephyr. Seeing that she was genuinely drifting off to sleep, Iggy rolled up his sleeve and made a dramatic, theatrical warding gesture with his soil-stained fingers—a cheeky half-hex to keep her pinned to the bench—before dropping through the hatch after the squad. Ignatius hurried down last, feeling distinctly out of place among the research equipment.

Down in the lower laboratory, Rill rushed to a heavy, reinforced containment safe mounted in the corner. She spun the combination dials, threw back the steel latch, and retrieved a small glass vial filled with a luminous, swirling neon liquid.

The glowing fluid inside was the exact iridescent hue as the mysterious substance Britt had discovered in the buried cache where they had recovered the compass.

Rill turned around, holding the vial up to the light. "Britt... does this liquid look familiar to you?" <!-- L1092 -->

Britt stared at the shimmering contents, her pulse spiking. "Yeah," Britt said grimly. "That looks identical to the substance that caused the fungal rash spreading on my shoulder." <!-- L1093 -->

"Not good," Rill muttered, her voice trembling as she gestured frantically toward an examination stool. "Not good at all! Sit down, please—sit down right now!" <!-- L1096 -->

Rill reached into her medical kit and pulled out a second glass vial, this one filled with a dense, shimmering neon-purple tonic. She shoved it into Britt's hands.

"Drink this immediately," Rill commanded urgently.

"What is this?" Britt asked, looking down at the purple draught.

Rill took a deep, shaky breath, leaning against the lab counter. "I was in Professor Ink's laboratory when the field sample was brought in from the ruins. It completely gutted me to recognize it... because this substance comes from the canyon territory where I was raised. Back before the clan exchange that I still can't talk about."

Rill pointed at the glowing vial, her eyes wide with terror. "You were exposed to a lethal biological agent that grows in only one isolated canyon gorge. It has no known permanent cure. But if you drink this stabilizing tonic once every seven days, it halts the cellular degradation. Because if you don't drink this, Britt... you are going to die in about two weeks."

Britt stared down at the vial, the weight of the diagnosis pressing heavily into her chest. The memory of the ancient vision echoed in her mind—she had seen others suffering from this same wasting illness in the memories of the old world, people who had asked dangerous questions and left the forest canopy.

"Has this substance ever come into contact with the Mizizi clan before?" Britt asked carefully. <!-- L1111 -->

"That should be impossible," Rill answered, shaking her head vigorously. "It grows in only one forbidden canyon sector, and no one ever goes there!" <!-- L1112 -->

"I think I carry memories of our people struggling with this exact condition," Britt pressed, her green eyes boring into Rill's. "Not necessarily the liquid itself, but the illness it causes." <!-- L1115 -->

Rill stared at her, baffled. "That makes no sense at all. It shouldn't be inside the Mizizi forest. Why would it be here? Why would it do the exact same thing to you that it does to the canyon folk?!" <!-- L1116 -->

"Where does it come from?" Britt asked. "Was it manufactured, or does it grow naturally?"

"It isn't manufactured," Rill whispered. "It just grows."

Rill stepped over to the laboratory door, slid the heavy iron deadbolt shut, and turned to face the room. Only the four of them remained inside: Rill, Britt, Aggie, and Iggy, with Ignatius standing quietly near the ladder rungs.

Ignatius shifted uncomfortably, his cuffs flickering with low embers. "I know how hard it is to talk about clans that the exchange scrubbed out of our heads," Ignatius murmured softly. "The indoctrination is something I'm still trying to overcome every single day."
