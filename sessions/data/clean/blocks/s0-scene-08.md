## THE CALL OF THE HORIZON

Plastered across every stone pillar, brass lamppost, and warehouse gate along the harbor, the recruitment broadsheets blared the same feverish promise: <!-- L0602 -->

*COME BE AN EXPLORER. THE TIME IS NOW. THE WORLD IS OPEN.*

It was a siren song that had lured a hundred thousand hopefuls to the edge of the southern sound. In the taverns and registration esplanades, the rumors of the Academy's brutal intake process were on everyone's lips. There were barely ten thousand berths in the incoming class; ninety thousand candidates would be washed out before their boots ever touched an airship deck. <!-- L0604 --> <!-- L0606 -->

According to the proctors and veterans milling through the queues, the Academy culled its numbers in three distinct phases.

First came the trials — a battery of evaluations designed to test a candidate's temperament, moral reaction under pressure, and raw aptitude, generating an initial tier ranking and assigning an explorer curriculum role. Second were the general lecture halls, where thousands would be packed into cavernous auditoriums in a grueling stall phase. And finally came the crucible: the day-one internships, where candidates were assigned to crews and forced to convince seasoned expedition captains to take them on. The captains gave the grades; if a candidate couldn't pull their weight on a real vessel, the Academy cut them loose. <!-- L0610 -->

Along the crowded registration desks, candidates received their official numbers and filed their preliminary profiles. Ignatius took his slip stamped as candidate 82804, his flaming hair catching the light as he answered probing questions about crisis command and authority — feeling less like a student and more like a recruit in a psychological gauntlet. <!-- L0634 --> <!-- L0643 -->

Across the concourse, Britt and Aggie stood side by side, registering their clan gifts into the imperial rosters, their complementary traits already hinting at the balance between botanical instinct and empathic perception. <!-- L0651 --> <!-- L0685 -->

Lomi logged his credentials with the easy confidence of a boilermaker who knew the guts of the citadel better than the clerks, while Iggy hovered near the outskirts of the queue, muttering under his heavy sailor's coat and warily watching the open sky.

By nightfall, the preliminary rolls were locked. None of the five had been turned away at the threshold. Their origins were wildly divergent, their cultures completely foreign to one another, but the imperial registers had logged them all into the incoming cohort. <!-- L0701 --> <!-- L0703 -->

The intake had begun.

Ahead of them, across the esplanade, stood the towering zinc hangars where tomorrow's formal testing floor awaited — where Britt would soon find herself staring down at a stubborn brass flag, preparing to knock over a desk so her cousin wouldn't be left behind in the dark.
