## THE STRANGERS ON THE WHARF

The citadel of Vumbua was a roaring labyrinth of half a million souls, swollen past bursting by the hundred thousand candidates converging upon its gates. <!-- L0451 -->

Down along the commercial quays where the southern barges unloaded, the bay was a forest of masts and steam funnels. It was here, beneath the slime-crusted pilings of the outer wharf, that the dark water began to churn.

A pair of small, soil-caked hands broke the surface, clutching the barnacled wood with frantic desperation.

Iggy hauled himself out of the brine, coughing and spitting dark harbor silt. His small humanoid frame was composed of packed loam, gravel, and damp clay, the edges of his joints crumbling and shedding gray grit onto the sun-bleached planks. To Iggy, the surface world was a nightmare of blinding light and terrifying desiccation. The afternoon sun beat down upon his clay skin like an open furnace, causing his porous shoulders to hiss and steam almost immediately. <!-- L0466 --> <!-- L0468 -->

"Too hot, too dry, bad ceiling, no ceiling," he muttered furiously, his voice a gravelly squeak.

Panicking, he whipped out a brass syringe from his leather kit and injected a squirt of oily mineral fluid directly into the side-valves of his oversized copper goggles, pressurizing his bulbous, bug-like eyes before the coastal breeze could dry them into cracked glass. He scrambled low against the deck, scooping up handfuls of wet harbor sludge and smearing it over his chest like a desperate layer of mud sunscreen. Every few yards he spun around, using his foot to kick harbor debris over his wet tracks — terrified that someone would trace his path back to the subterranean fissure. <!-- L0475 --> <!-- L0483 --> <!-- L0487 -->

Sitting on a nearby bollard was an old, weather-beaten sailor with a walrus mustache, nursing a clay jug. The sailor watched the little mud-creature hiss, steam, and kick dust with the mild, unbothered curiosity of a man who had sailed forty years and seen stranger things washed up on tide-lines.

Slowly, the old sailor set down his jug, unbuttoned his massive, dark wool trench coat — a garment stiff with grease, salt, and years of sea spray — and casually dropped it over Iggy's head. <!-- L0494 --> <!-- L0496 -->

The heavy fabric collapsed across Iggy's shoulders like a collapsing mine shaft.

Iggy froze. The crushing, suffocating weight of the soaked wool pressed downward upon his spine. It was hot, it was rough, and to a creature born in the deep pressure of the subterranean dark, it was pure, unadulterated heaven. He let out a long, shuddering sigh of contentment, his clay frame settling under the welcome burden. He blinked through his copper lenses, adjusted the enormous drooping collar around his chin, and waddled toward the city street like a sentient lump of damp soil wrapped in a blanket. <!-- L0500 --> <!-- L0504 -->

A hundred yards up the waterfront, another arrival stepped off the passenger ferry from the eastern straits.

Ignatius stepped onto the cobblestones, his soot-stained traveler's pack slung over one shoulder. A crown of pale, dancing embers curled through his dark hair, flickering against the ocean breeze. In a harbor as cosmopolitan as Vumbua, where sailors from a dozen frontier ports mingled with trade envoys, an Emberkin barely raised an eyebrow. <!-- L0514 -->

He stood wide-eyed, taking in the towering masts, the shrieking gulls, and the dizzying expanse of the city. He was the new kid on the block, head spinning, entirely unsure of how this bustling metropolis worked.

Back home on the caldera, his elders had grown exhausted by his teenage defiance. He had been caught sneaking into off-limits volcanic vents, refusing to accept that the mountain's rekindled warmth came from foreign batteries rather than true flame. Fed up with his insubordination, the clan council had banished him across the water to take the Academy trials.

*They said I was running away,* he thought, adjusting the strap of his worn bag as he looked up toward the great entrance arches. *They called it fleeing. But it isn't flight. It's a pilgrimage.* <!-- L0516 --> <!-- L0522 -->

He was going to enter their trials. He was going to become an adventurer. And out on the wild edges of the uncharted world, he would find the true source of primal heat for his people — and prove to the elders that the mountain didn't need foreign sugar to burn. <!-- L0524 -->
