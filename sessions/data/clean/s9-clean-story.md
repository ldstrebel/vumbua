## CHAPTER 82: THE LOGISTICS OFFICER

The night before the Loom, before the slates, before any of it — the *Zephyr* had been a different kind of world. <!-- FLASHBACK -->

The VIP deck existed in its own atmosphere, partitioned from the racing spectators below by three floors of polished brass fittings, reinforced glass galleries, and the particular silence that comes when you have paid enough to make noise optional. The mahogany bar at the aft end of the lounge gleamed under oil-lamp chandeliers that swayed with the slow oscillation of the dirigible's hull. Beyond the gallery windows, the Apex Arena was a lit canyon far below — fire and speed and the roar of the crowd a muffled thing from this altitude, more felt than heard. **[[Dee]] (Arena Logistics Officer):** "It's impossible! I tell you, there's no way that we can reset the ring in a day. It's preposterous! I mean, look at the carnage they're wreaking down there!" <!-- L0059 -->

Loami stood behind the bar, wiping the rim of a glass with the practiced efficiency of a man who had spent several years making other people's problems look like opportunities. He watched Dee — an Arena Logistics Officer in a black dress that had been elegant four rounds of drinks ago — slump over her empty glass and wave at something invisible in the middle distance. He refilled it and slid it across the polished wood surface without saying anything yet. <!-- L0057 --> <!-- L0061 -->

"I mean, they expect me to just find all these materials and stuff and set them out in the sand." Dee pressed her palm flat against the bar, as if the bar might validate her. "I just don't understand *why!*" <!-- L0063 -->

"What materials, Dee?" Loami said. <!-- L0065 -->

She pulled a rolled parchment manifest from inside her coat and spread it across the bar. It was a dense document — sector coordinates, equipment weights, material categories, installation sequences. Five distinct regions of the Apex Ring's canyon floor were delineated, each one annotated with a list of specific industrial items to be seeded: canvas tarps, high-tension bungees, rusted iron grappling hooks, fuel bladders, raw crystal formations, bamboo stockpiles. <!-- L0067 -->

"Dee," Loami said carefully, studying the manifest. "Know why they need these materials?" <!-- L0069 -->

"I don't know." She reached for her glass. "But they always make squads after the first year, so it makes sense they're going to put them to some test or something." <!-- L0071 -->

The words hit Loami like a spark catching tinder. That was exactly how Vumbua operated. Every year, candidates were sorted into squads following the written exam — and the long-standing rumor among harbor mechanics and working-class crew was that these squads were never truly random. The natural harmonic resonances forged between candidates during their first week of contact — shared pressure, shared humor, the particular frequency of people who have kept each other upright through chaos — those echoes didn't simply evaporate. The Loom picked them up.

Loami studied the five sector coordinates on the manifest. And then, quietly, behind his expression, he began to think. <!-- L0073 --> <!-- L0075 -->

Ignatious — volatile, burning, quick. Britt — dense and patient as good timber, roots going deeper than she showed. Aggie — watching everything, storing everything, the fungal wisdom of someone who learned by being part of a larger network. Iggy — secretive and clay-deep and strange in the best way, carrying things from underground that hadn't been brought into sunlight yet.

*These four,* he thought. *And me. We are already Squad 907. We just don't have the name yet.*

He looked at the manifest again. Five sectors. Five candidates who were already, functionally, a crew. <!-- L0075 -->

This was useful information.

## CHAPTER 83: THE LOOM DROP

Friday morning, the grandstands. <!-- L0081 -->

Dean Isolde Vane stood atop the high podium overlooking the canyon. Behind her, the fifty-thousand-seat amphitheater was full — first-year candidates packed into the tiered rows, their slates still warm from the morning's exam results. The window-washer steam-lifts rose along the basalt cliff walls in a slow, mechanical procession, rising to rim height and holding there, waiting. <!-- L0081 -->

"Cadets of Vumbua!" Her voice carried across the basin without amplification — a feature of the arena's acoustics, engineered specifically for this moment every year. "Your written results are recorded. Your first year begins now. Behind me lies the Apex Ring. Down on that floor, your squads must prove your worth before the Senior Captains. Your voyage depends on what you build and what you discover." <!-- L0083 -->

As the exam telepathic suppression ward lifted — a subtle sensation, like a window being opened in a sealed room — Aggie turned immediately toward Britt, her voice arriving through their shared fungal channel with the tentative gentleness of someone checking in after something difficult. <!-- L0085 -->

*Britt. Are you feeling healthy enough to proceed? There's no rush for us to get back to the forest tonight — if we don't make it back for a week or more, it will be totally fine. But I can talk to plants. If I go to one of the zones with a lot of plant life, I can find out quickly where things are.* <!-- L0087 -->

*Got it,* Britt sent back. *Aggie — deliver the note to Iggy, then head toward Sector 3 to scout the plants. We've got things to do, and you've got things to do.* <!-- L0089 -->

Aggie nodded, physically, to no one in particular, and moved off through the crowded rows toward Iggy's seat. <!-- L0091 -->

At the upper grandstands, Loami had already found his way to the security perimeter, where the temporary firefighter brigade brought in for crowd management was finishing its morning rotation. He scanned the faces until he found one he knew — a fellow from his UN Maintenance days back home, one of the harbor firefighter crew. <!-- L0093 -->

"Hey." He leaned over the railing. "You're going to see a Humazi and a little stone-looking guy together down there. Give this to them." He held up a folded note. <!-- L0095 -->

The firefighter looked at him. Then at the note. Then at Loami. "Loami? You know your gold's no good for me, buddy. There's only one thing I want." <!-- L0099 -->

"All right, fine." Loami reached into his coat and produced a bottle — dark glass, sealed with cork, containing approximately one liter of something he had brewed personally in the Zephyr's galley space over the last three nights. He set it on the rail alongside the remainder of his tip earnings from the night before. "I made this at the bar the other day. It's literally every tip I got. But it's worth it." <!-- L0101 --> <!-- L0103 -->

The firefighter picked up the bottle, looked at it with the serious expression of a connoisseur, and then looked back up. "All right! You know what — for you, I'll do it." He studied the note. "But two Mizizi and a stone?" <!-- L0105 -->

"A little stone-looking guy." Loami indicated with his hands approximately Iggy's height and width. "He's tiny. He's got goggles on. You can't miss him." <!-- L0107 -->

"Okay. All right. I'll see what I can do." The firefighter extended a hand. "Good luck on the test!" <!-- L0109 -->

"Thanks. I'm feeling pretty lucky." <!-- L0111 -->

The firefighter turned back to his squad and, with the authority of a senior crew member making an executive decision, announced: "Yes, they got this handled right here! Nothing to worry about over here. What are we even doing? Let's go find some Mizizi!" <!-- L0113 --> <!-- L0115 -->

The entire corner of firefighter security guards abandoned their assigned section in a body and streamed off across the grandstand. The area they left behind was completely unmonitored and would remain so for the foreseeable future.

Loami watched this with the calm of a man who knew exactly what he was looking at. He turned back toward the lifts.

Two rows below and to the left, a disturbance had erupted near the front of the failed-candidate section. Ludo Castellan was already on his feet, his voice carrying the distinctive pitch of someone who had graduated past mere complaint into something approaching philosophical outrage. <!-- L0119 -->

"This is preposterous! We demand a re-evaluation!" <!-- L0121 -->

A security guard responded with the direct simplicity of the Arena Guard: "Shut up! *Shut up!* Stop whining. Do better on the next test!" <!-- L0123 -->

Ten guards converged on the Castellan party. The resulting commotion was substantial. It created an excellent distraction. <!-- L0125 -->

The steam-lifts descended. <!-- L0125 -->

## CHAPTER 84: THE PIXIE AND THE GRAVITY ROAD

Two sections over from Britt, the exam slate in front of Ignatious had been staring at a completed exam. Completed some time ago, based on the warmth of his arms crossed over it. The exam had, by his assessment, been very fast — you were either right or you were wrong, and while he had been wrong on a lot of them, there was a useful clarity in that. <!-- L0131 --> <!-- L0139 -->

He was asleep.

He came back to consciousness the same way he always did — the fire-kin equivalent of waking up, which is that his literal flame hair sparked into active combustion and began flickering with warm amber light. He sat up, rubbed the back of his neck, and looked around at the noise and motion of 50,000 people simultaneously reacting to their exam results.

"Yes!" he said, to no one in particular. "Yes, I won! I touched the Obelisk!" He blinked at the grandstand walls, recalibrating. "Where am I?" <!-- L0133 -->

Britt appeared at his shoulder. "Did you take your exam?" <!-- L0137 -->

"I think so!" He was, he felt, broadly correct about this. "It was a very fast exam, you know? You're right or you're wrong. I've been wrong on a lot of them. But that's fine. It's fine." <!-- L0139 -->

"Yeah," Britt said. "You're not wrong. Okay." She took him by the hand and moved toward the lifts with the focused, non-negotiable energy of someone who has already spent their morning rescuing one squadmate from a harbor basin and is not slowing down now. <!-- L0141 -->

As they moved laterally across the grandstand rows toward the Sector 2 lift cluster, a dwarf arena attendant was shouting the challenge briefing at anyone within audible range, which turned out to be most of the east tier:

"Okay! *Okay!* Maybe they should listen to me! So — arena is your first week of class, but really it's just your first day of class. Whatever. They want you to get down there and show them what you can do with what you actually *do,* not just what you can learn and remember on a test! So your first test: get across the arena. They have dinner waiting for you on the other side! It's night. There's stuff down there. Try not to get hurt. If you do get hurt, medical staff will be in gliders to pull you out. It will cost you credits though — academy credits, points towards your graduation — so *don't do it!* And they also told me to say: at 8:00 AM tomorrow there is a challenge, and if you're still in the arena at that time, you are eligible for the challenge. It is very dangerous! They recommend evacuating at no later than 5:00 AM if you do not want to participate. Again, they will rescue you, but people have *died* because we cannot protect you from everything — even your own stupidity, especially that last one! I think that's everything I'm supposed to say. Good luck!" <!-- L0145 -->

Britt and Ignatious stepped onto the fourth steam-lift as it began its descent. As the platform dropped past the rim, a fine layer of charged static passed through them — the air pressure changed, sand particles floated, their ears popped — and the crowd noise above simply stopped. Not faded. Stopped. <!-- L0147 -->

Each of them heard only their own heartbeat.

Britt heard something else first: a sharp, high-pitched squeak from below the platform's edge.

She looked over the railing. A tiny pixie cadet — barely the height of Britt's knee, wings tucked in terror, frozen on the canyon floor directly beneath the descending lift counterweights — was staring up at her with the frozen expression of someone who had just realized they had made a significant miscalculation. <!-- L0149 -->

Britt didn't deliberate. She dropped onto her stomach on the lift deck, reached under the platform edge, grabbed the pixie cadet by the back of their collar with both hands, and threw them sideways across the landing zone with enough force to clear the weight path by six feet. <!-- L0151 --> <!-- L0153 -->

From the sidelines, a spectator cadet who had been watching: "Holy — is that *worse* or better than getting crushed?!" <!-- L0155 -->

Britt was already back on her feet. She rolled her shoulders. "Okay. Let me go." <!-- L0157 -->

The lift hit the canyon floor. <!-- L0157 -->

## CHAPTER 85: AIR SHOPPING

On the canyon floor, Ignatious looked across the expanse of the Apex Ring — its biomes stretching in every direction, its gravity corridors already flickering with visible distortion at the distant canyon walls — and made a decision. <!-- L0163 -->

"Hey, let's go check out the far side of the arena!" <!-- L0165 -->

The ankle wings ignited. They were small things — winged flame constructs that formed at his boot heels when he needed them — but they were efficient. He activated them, grabbed Britt under her arms, and launched straight up into open air. <!-- L0167 -->

"*What?!*" <!-- L0169 -->

"We're taking the air route!" Ignatious called, angling toward the far sector. "We're air shopping, baby!" <!-- L0171 -->

The gravity corridor hit them within thirty seconds.

The Apex Ring's gravity-scrambled canyon zones were not metaphorical. Directional gravity simply changed — down became sideways became up became a diagonal ripping current that had no name in any physical language Ignatious spoke. The air routes through the central corridor were the fastest path between sectors, and also the most violent. An outcrop appeared sideways out of the air as gravity flipped. He clipped it hard — ten HP of raw impact — and burned two prayer dice absorbing the collision down to nine, staying on trajectory through sheer stubborn will. <!-- L0173 -->

"There!" Britt called from under his arm, pointing. "I see bamboo thickets and supply piles near Sector 2!" <!-- L0175 -->

"I will take," Ignatious announced, with the focused inventory management of a man conducting a very fast transaction, "fuel bladders, leaky coolant hoses, heavy tar buckets, hollow bamboo tubes, and a sponge boom block! Thank you!" <!-- L0177 -->

"Leaking coolant hoses for the raft, please!" <!-- L0179 -->

He banked into a low sweep. Britt reached down from his grip, skimming the supply piles at speed, and scooped: a full bushel of hollow bamboo, four fuel bladders, a bundle of coolant hoses, two tar buckets, and one sponge boom block — all gathered in a pass that lasted approximately four seconds and required only that she not let go of him while doing it. <!-- L0181 -->

He burned a point of Hope to clear her stress. The load was heavy. She barely noticed.

They angled toward Sector 3 with a full equipment haul, leaving a small red contrail across the canyon sky.

## CHAPTER 86: THE STEAM VENT SPA

Sector 4 was a landscape that smelled exactly like the inside of a geothermal fissure, which Iggy found deeply, personally welcoming. <!-- L0187 -->

He dropped off the Sector 4 lift into a haze of roaring steam vents, sulfurous deposits, and wet mineral heat. The ground was fractured basalt with glowing crack-lines where geothermal pressure leaked upward. The air was forty degrees warmer than the canyon floor above and smelled of sulfur in a way that was, for Iggy, essentially the smell of comfort.

"'S warm down here," he said. "Best thing Iggy's ever smelt in his life." <!-- L0189 -->

He walked directly toward the nearest billowing steam vent and stood in it. This caused 2 HP of heat damage to his fragile wizard frame, which he noted with the detachment of a person who considers this a reasonable exchange rate for the feeling of being deeply, completely hydrated. His clay skin drank the steam in through its pores and he stood there for approximately three minutes, deeply content. <!-- L0191 -->

Near a sub-fissure in the rock, he crouched and collected several dense clay plugs — compressed earth pushed up by geothermal pressure, high quality, structurally excellent — and sealed them in his knapsack. One stress to extract. Worth it. <!-- L0191 -->

He looked up.

Standing over the highest-quality crystal formation in the sector — a cluster of raw sulfur energy crystals with a high-purity glow — was an 18-foot Cyclops Troll, holding approximately the world's most impressive handful of raw crystals and regarding them with the proprietary satisfaction of someone who got here first. <!-- L0193 -->

Iggy studied the situation. He studied the crystals. He studied the troll.

He studied the crystals again.

"Hey!" he called up. "Big guy! Size ain't everything with crystals, ya know!" <!-- L0195 -->

The troll looked down at him. The troll's single eye, roughly the size of Iggy's entire torso, focused with visible irritation.

"Hey, hey, hey!" The troll's voice was somewhere between thunder and a very annoyed large person. "What are you doing? I got the *best* crystals! I did not make a mistake!" <!-- L0199 -->

Iggy began climbing the troll's back with the methodical efficiency of a very small creature that has already committed to the bit. <!-- L0197 -->

"Size isn't always better!" he called, from approximately the troll's shoulder. "Feel the *power!*" <!-- L0201 -->

"Yes it is!" The troll attempted to reach back and get a hand on Iggy. "*With* sulfur crystals, they both have a stronger aromatic and sulfuric presence, and *that's* what we need! No thanks! Your crystals, my crystals! *Your* crystals, *my* crystals!" <!-- L0203 -->

"You can have one of my crystals," Iggy offered reasonably, "in exchange for one of yours!" <!-- L0205 -->

"I don't negotiate!" The troll's free hand located Iggy, closed around him, and lifted him ten feet into the air. "Your crystals, my crystals!" <!-- L0207 -->

Iggy, at ten feet elevation with his arms pinned, made one rapid assessment of the situation, leaned forward as far as the grip allowed, and bit the troll directly on the hand.

The troll shrieked — high, sharp, completely surprised — and dropped everything: Iggy, the handful of raw crystals, his composure. <!-- L0209 --> <!-- L0211 -->

Iggy fell eight feet and hit the rock, which cost him two HP and one stress that he burned on impact. He fired his Mud Lash from the ground — a whip of dense clay and soil that shot upward and snapped around the largest raw crystal before it could bounce away. He reeled it in. <!-- L0211 -->

He looked at his pouch of smaller crystals. Looked at the crystal in his mud-whip hand. Left one of the small ones on the rock as what he decided to classify as an apology present. <!-- L0215 -->

From nearby, a female Cyclops NPC who had been watching the entire exchange screamed in fury. "*Sam!* He *bit* you!" Her eyes turned blood-red. <!-- L0217 --> <!-- L0219 -->

Iggy chose not to stay and experience what came next. He tucked his giant raw crystal under one arm, tucked his clay plugs under the other, and left Sector 4 at his maximum possible velocity, which was surprisingly fast for a creature that was technically shaped like a small clay sphere. <!-- L0221 -->

## CHAPTER 87: THE OVERCLOCK

Sector 1's gravity canyons were the most technically demanding terrain in the Apex Ring — not because of what they contained, but because of what they did to direction. The canyon walls generated overlapping gravitational fields that shifted every thirty to ninety seconds, pulling explorers left, then down, then sideways, then diagonally. Standing still in a Sector 1 corridor was theoretically possible and practically suicidal. <!-- L0225 -->

Loami moved through them at speed, Spider-Man-style — running along the right wall when gravity pulled left, transferring to the left wall when it pulled right, jumping between surfaces at the exact moment each shift changed polarity. He took five HP of damage from a mistimed transfer and rolled through the impact, which reduced it to survivable, and kept moving. <!-- L0227 -->

At the far end of Sector 1, where the terrain opened into the edge of Sector 5, the ground disappeared. <!-- L0229 -->

The Sector 5 fall tunnel was exactly what the name implied: a vertical shaft 200 feet deep, dropping straight down into a deep basin pool that connected to the main harbor basin below. The walls of the shaft were lined with scrap shelves — materials cached at various depths for candidates with the nerve to grab them on the way down. Gravity here pulled straight and constant and merciless toward the water below. <!-- L0229 -->

There was no way around. There was only through.

Loami looked at the shaft. Then he activated Overclock. <!-- L0231 -->

The ability pushed his mechanical body into maximum output — every system running simultaneously at a power level that generated exactly one stress marker per use and could not be sustained indefinitely. He felt his joints heat and his reflexes sharpen to a pitch just below painful. He took one step to the edge and dove. <!-- L0233 -->

"*AAAAAAAHHHHHHH!*" <!-- L0235 -->

The fall was 200 feet at Overclock speed, which meant he had approximately two and a half seconds to do everything else. He grabbed a heavy canvas tarp from a shelf at the 80-foot mark, snagged a set of rusted iron grappling hooks from a niche at the 120-foot mark, left behind a clutch of lift-stone chunks that were too unstable to be useful, and hit the basin water at the speed of a person who had made a choice and was fully committed to it. <!-- L0237 -->

A ton of metal scrap cascaded in after him from the disturbed shelves.

He surfaced. He rubbed his head. He was missing one armor slot, had taken a minor wound, and was completely soaked from crown to boot. The canvas tarp was intact. The grappling hooks were intact. The bungees he'd managed to grab on the way down were intact. <!-- L0239 -->

He grinned.

*Long game.*

## CHAPTER 88: SECTOR 3 RENDEZVOUS

Dusk fell over the Apex Ring in slow stages — the canyon walls cooling from gold to amber to grey, the temperature dropping into the basin from above while the geothermal zones in Sectors 4 and 5 pushed heat upward in the other direction. The sky above the canyon rim narrowed to a stripe of darkening blue. <!-- L0245 -->

Iggy emerged from the tree line of Sector 3 moving at a shuffling sprint that was not quite fast enough to be reassuring, his crystal under one arm and his clay plugs tucked into his coat. He looked up at the darkening sky and caught a streak of red moving low across the canopy — Ignatious carrying Britt, trailing a contrail of harvested materials and the faint smell of fuel bladder seals. <!-- L0245 -->

"*Over here!* Got the big crystal!" <!-- L0247 -->

They landed in the Sector 3 clearing with a soft crunch of stone and fuel bladder and bamboo. Moments later, Loami emerged from the water channel that connected the Sector 5 basin to the Sector 3 margins — dripping, grinning, hauling a canvas tarp in one hand and a bundle of grappling hooks in the other. Seaweed. Minor wound. Completely unbothered. <!-- L0249 -->

"Canvas tarp, bungees, grappling hooks secured," he said. "Aggie's already ahead of us in the wetlands setting up plant node alignment." <!-- L0251 -->

"We've got fuel bladders, coolant hoses, bamboo, sponge boom." Britt ran her hand across the haul Ignatious had stacked on the clearing floor. "Phase 1 scavenge complete." <!-- L0253 -->

The five candidates of Squad 907 stood in the clearing as the first stars appeared above the canyon rim. The haul spread around them on the ground — canvas and grappling iron and raw sulfur crystal and hollow bamboo and fuel rubber and compressed clay plugs and enough bungee cord to lash together something that might, with ingenuity and whatever Aggie found in the wetland network, become something extraordinary. <!-- L0255 -->

Above the canyon, the 8:00 AM challenge waited, with everything that would follow it.

But for now: Phase 1 complete. One squad. Five people. Tomorrow was a different problem.
