As their palms touched the petrified wood, the clearing at Center Grove dissolved into a swirling mist of Aetheric light.

It was not a dream, nor a simple illusion. It felt like stepping into the memory of a stranger—seeing through eyes long since turned to dust, feeling the secondhand warmth of fires lit a thousand years before. The vision belonged to Mwaza-Kasa, stored within the quiet, immortal shell of the spirit tortoise, watching the world of Vumbua move backward through time like water flowing back up a mountain.

Ignatius gasped as he felt himself standing in another man's skin, looking out upon the world before the Veil, before the clans grew cold and isolated in their separate groves. "Harmony is going in reverse," Ignatius whispered through the shared vision, his voice echoing in the vast silence. "Harmony is like the clans before they stagnated."

Through the tortoise's ancient, unblinking gaze, the visions unspooled backward across the centuries:

*First came the Spark of Fire and Wood.* Ignatius felt the heat of an Ash-Blood forge burning against his chest, yet he stood before a colossal wooden archway carved by Mizizi hands. Beside him, an ancient Mizizi shaper pressed a glowing ember to the raw petrified timber, and the wood did not burn—it burst into a radiant, living lattice of amber light. *"The tree remembers the fire,"* a voice echoed across the centuries. *"The fire remembers the tree."* Two distinct peoples, joining their sparks in an explosion of mutual creation—shaping doors that never rotted and hearths that never grew cold.

*Then came the Tempest of the Sky.* Zephyr felt the roar of wind against her face, looking out from a high basalt cliff. High above the clouds, wild Fulgur riders soared upon the backs of colossal, four-winged dragonflies, their spears crackling with blue arc-lightning. Below them, ancient stone-builders constructed heavy basalt fortresses. At first, there was war—the Fulgur saw the stone walls as a cage built to trap their tempests. But then a Mizizi elder stepped between them, planting a tall copper-bound rod into the earth. *"A wall does not trap the wind—it stops the wind from tearing down the nursery,"* the elder spoke. *"A lightning rod doesn't trap the storm... it channels it to light the dark."*

*Then came the Abyssal Roots.* Iggy felt the tremendous, crushing weight of the deep ocean, looking through the eyes of a Trench-Kin ancestor walking upon the sea floor. Glowing blue lichen illuminated colossal drowned ziggurats where Trench-Kin artisans carved basalt anchors to ground the shifting tectonic plates, tethering the land to the ocean bed.

*Then came the Surging Canyon Waters.* Rill felt the spray of cool mountain water against her cheeks as a dry, crumbling desert canyon in the Wadi territory suddenly burst with life. Hidden subterranean springs surged through carved stone aqueducts, turning dying ruins into lush, terraced gardens.

*And at the center of it all was the Unbroken Web.* High above Vumbua, the five clans were not isolated islands behind closed barriers. A single, unbroken root-and-mycelium network bound the world into a living, resonant equilibrium. Every spark—fire, wood, lightning, earth, and water—fed into the same continuous cycle, stabilizing the Aetheric decay.

Ignatius watched the ancient network pulse, the profound truth settling into his chest. They had not been built to live apart. The decay had begun the moment the clans locked their gates and forgot each other.
