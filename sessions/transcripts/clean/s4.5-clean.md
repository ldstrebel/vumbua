# Session 4.5: Reality Anchors & Dead Pigs

## Processing the Night Before

In the early hours of the morning, Brit and Aggie returned to their dorms in Block 12, shaken by the previous night’s violent physical altercation. The incident left Brit flabbergasted, struggling to comprehend the hostility and malice of the outside world compared to the deep-rooted trust and non-violent culture of the Mizi clan. In the clan, major conflicts arise from the wilderness or the madness of the fungal passages, not from malicious theft. They slept fitfully, waiting for the morning horns.

## Breakfast and "Dead Pigs"

The horns woke the dormitories, displaying the day's schedule: Reality Anchoring and Aetheric Defense. As the Block 12 "green room" dorms stirred to life, Aggie recognized a familiar face from the bonfire—Bramble, a towering, wood-like student.

Bramble was accompanied by a lively group:
* **Pip:** An eccentric, hyper-energetic gnome girl absolutely obsessed with the dining hall's biscuits and fresh butter.
* **Kael:** A shy, frail, nerdy boy with a knack for tinkering.
* **Saffron:** A quiet, ethereal artist who constantly sketched her surroundings in a notebook.

The newly formed group headed to the massive Academy dining hall. Along the way, Kael explained his current project: creating "Speaking Stones." He described how all Harmony Tech is powered by "resonance," a river of energy that flows through the world but must be carefully restricted by valuable Umber Crystals to prevent explosions.

At the buffet, cultural differences clashed once more. While Brit stuck to a meager Mizi-style diet of fruits and nuts, Aggie bravely tried an unfamiliar pastry. The group's dynamic took a comedic turn when Pip excitedly announced she was eating a "dead pig" (bacon). The casual consumption of dead animals bewildered Brit, highlighting the profound differences between Mizi survival and Academy luxury.

## Professor Thorne and Reality Anchoring

The first class of the day, Reality Anchoring, was taught in a massive 5,000-person amphitheater by the young, disheveled Professor Silas Thorne. Thorne presented a bleak concept: the "edge of the world" is a destructive force constantly encroaching on Harmony, and "Reality Anchors" are necessary to port resonance and keep the end at bay.

He described a terrifying "dead island"—a place where civilization once thrived but now lacked reality anchors. There, matter remained (such as skeletons and buildings), but energy vanished completely. It was a vacuum where Harmony tech died instantly, and there was no sense of cyclical rebirth, only finality. 

This theory challenged the core Mizi belief that *not dying* is the end of civilization. Brit and Aggie realized with horror that the Mizi forest's recent resistance to dying and pruning might be an inverse parallel to the dead island phenomenon.

Frustrated by the vast class's lack of attention, Professor Thorne abruptly closed his book and walked out mid-lecture.

## The Pursuit

Unwilling to miss out on learning, Pip jumped onto Bramble's shoulders, and the group (minus Saffron, who remained behind to sketch) chased Thorne through the sprawling Academy hallways. Guided by Bramble's height and Pip's directions, they managed to catch the professor.

When cornered, Thorne elaborated on his theories. He argued against the Mizi perspective, stating that people who join Harmony and avoid death are actually *better off* (citing the Ashbloods as an example). He ultimately agreed to post a study guide for the class before departing to prepare for his next lecture.

## Seeking Valentine Sterling

Following their second class, Aetheric Defense—which focused on armoring ships against gargantuan Aether Beasts—Aggie split off from the new group to track down Val for tutoring. She borrowed one of Kael’s prototype speaking stones to test communications later.

Using her keen instincts, Aggie located Val heading toward his dorms. He was surrounded by a gaggle of sycophants, looking every bit the reluctant celebrity. As he arrived at the high-end Sterling Hall, he was welcomed inside by two intimidating, dark-haired relatives—the Castellan twins—who acted as his sentries, glaring down the remaining students before shutting the heavy doors.
