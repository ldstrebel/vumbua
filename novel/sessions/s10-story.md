## CHAPTER 89: MAGNETIC SAND SURFING

Sector 1's canyon sands moved.

Not in the way that wind moves sand — that gentle horizontal drift, patient and directional. The canyon sands of Sector 1 moved in waves: sudden vertical cliffs that solidified in seconds from loose grain to sheer packed stone, fifty-foot swells of compressed mineral matter that reared up without warning and slammed flat again before you could account for them. Moving through Sector 1 before reaching the Sector 3 rendezvous point required a different calculation than simple speed. <!-- L0036 -->

Loami worked the calculation with salvaged copper-wound industrial anchors and magnetic clamps. The friction of the sand against the canyon walls generated static electricity — slow-building, diffuse, but real — and the magnetic clamps could be temporarily charged by contact with the moving sands, creating brief bursts of polarity that could be exploited for traction, momentum, or, if you were good at the timing, something closer to surfing. <!-- L0037 -->

Ignatious had attempted to hold the magnets overhead as a flying spotter. The magnetic surge yanked him downward with considerably more force than anticipated and planted him face-first into a forty-foot sand wave. He emerged from this intact, if substantially coated in mineral dust. <!-- L0039 -->

They adapted. Loami mounted a sheet of scrap metal and used polarity flips — alternating the charge on the clamps every thirty to forty seconds as the sand-wave geometry changed — to skate across the canyon floor while Ignatious tracked from ten feet overhead, calling out the locations of embedded hardware as it became visible between wave crests. Iron-hooped barrels. Steel cables. Copper fittings. Loami harvested them all before the sand closed back over. <!-- L0041 -->

By the time they cleared Sector 1, the haul was substantial and Loami was completely covered in static-generated fringe effects: standing hair, sparking clamp-tips, and a thin corona of mineral dust that clung to every surface of his canvas collar and flat cap. He looked like a man who had been in close proximity to a lightning strike. He seemed entirely fine with this.

They moved toward Sector 3. <!-- L0041 -->

## CHAPTER 90: IGGY'S FIRST TREE

Sector 3 was where the Apex Ring stored its forest.

The Sector 3 tree line was a wall of petrified giants — ninety-foot trunks of compressed mineral-wood, their root systems sunk deep into the canyon bedrock, their upper canopy interlaced at forty to sixty feet above the ground in a lattice of pale grey branches. The trees were ancient. Not alive in the way living trees were alive, but not entirely dead either — something in the mineral-stone grain of them retained a memory of what they had been, a structural echo of growth patterns locked in rock ten thousand years ago. <!-- L0047 -->

Iggy had never seen a tree.

He stood at the edge of the Sector 3 tree line, looked up at the nearest ninety-foot trunk, and simply stopped moving. He had grown up in Bamboo — a city of compressed urban earth, manicured median plants, contained green things that knew their boundaries. He had seen images in books. He had not been prepared for what ninety feet of petrified wood looked like from the base.

He set down his gear. He pulled out his journal and began to sketch. He peeled bark samples from the lower trunk with careful, methodical hands, pressing them flat into the pages of his knapsack for later study. The bark was remarkable — layered in a way that implied process, patience, time. He was not going to stop now. <!-- L0049 -->

A frustrated roar rolled through the steam-vented clearing behind him. Sam the Cyclops Troll, still resentful about both the crystal and the bite, was closing the distance across Sector 3 at a very committed pace. <!-- L0051 -->

"Let's go, let's go!" Iggy said, to himself, packing his samples. <!-- L0053 -->

He navigated southwest through the dense woods.

**Navigation Attempt 1:** He walked for twenty minutes through the petrified forest, confident in his direction. The canopy brightened. He stepped out of the tree line directly back into the clearing he'd started from. Sam was now three-quarters of the distance closed. <!-- L0057 -->

**Navigation Attempt 2 (The Pinball Loop):** He tried again, angling more deliberately, bouncing off stone trunks that had the courtesy to redirect him without apology. He emerged from the forest line a second time — directly in front of Sam, Sarah the female Cyclops (arms crossed, smirking), and a young elf girl standing beside her. Sarah extended her open hand with elaborate patience. She wanted the crystal back. <!-- L0058 -->

"Hold please!" Iggy said.

He reached into his coat, produced and drank a stamina potion with the efficiency of a man conducting field maintenance, dropped into his rolling ball form — his body compressing to a dense, fast-moving sphere — and bolted for the basin water 100 yards away. <!-- L0062 -->

As he ran: "A peace offering!" He dropped his bark samples on the ground behind him without slowing. "A tiny version of an apology!" <!-- L0064 -->

Sam, still irritated, tracked him across the clearing and reached down with massive stone hands. He forgot to close his fingers. Iggy popped out from between them like a very determined bowling ball. Sam's expression suggested he was aware this was embarrassing. <!-- L0066 -->

He picked up a twenty-foot petrified tree trunk. He threw it.

The tree trunk crossed thirty yards in two seconds and hit the muddy riverbank six inches from Iggy, exploding dirt and shockwave into his side for six HP of physical damage. Iggy burned three stress to absorb the impact. The impact still happened. He hit the muddy riverbank, got up, and continued. <!-- L0066 --> <!-- L0068 -->

From this point forward, Iggy had a permanent traumatic phobia of trees. He made a mental note to think about this later and leaped off the bank into the 1,700-foot deep water basin to escape. <!-- L0070 --> <!-- L0072 -->

## CHAPTER 91: THE SOULLESS FOREST

Along the Sector 3 shoreline, Aggie and Britt held the rendezvous point and waited. <!-- L0078 -->

Aggie had walked through the forest already — a full scouting circuit through the Sector 3 mycelium maze using her fungal network sense. The maze was impressive, architecturally. The trees were correct in dimension, distribution, and species pattern. Everything about it was technically accurate.

"It looks like home," Aggie said. "But it's missing the soul. A master artist created this place knowing nothing about what it feels like to live in it." <!-- L0080 -->

She looked at the shoreline, scanning the basin surface. "Are we worried about anyone not being able to find us?" <!-- L0082 -->

"Nope," Britt said. <!-- L0084 -->

Aggie walked to an ancient petrified tree at the edge of the clearing and placed her palms flat against the bark. She channeled her Mizizi instinct — the fungal-root communication network, the same principle that allowed her to speak with Britt across distance — and sent the question into the wood. <!-- L0086 -->

*Tell me where our people are.*

The Instinct roll was a 17, with Hope burning clean. The forest answered — not in words, not in any language, but in vivid, concrete imagery pressed directly into her mind like hands pressing shapes into clay: *We were told not to tell you where your people were...* A pause that felt like institutional embarrassment. *But one of yours tried to enter the forest twice and got lost. We lost track after one of our brethren was felled and thrown at him.* <!-- L0088 --> <!-- L0090 -->

"Someone threw a tree?!" Aggie said, pulling her hands back. <!-- L0092 -->

She went to the shoreline. She found a flat basalt ledge at the water's edge and began tapping river stones together over the water — steady, rhythmic, repeating. A simple acoustic signal. A pattern with no particular meaning except: *here, here, here.* <!-- L0094 -->

Thirty feet below the surface, Iggy heard the clacking. He recognized it for what it was — the same instinct that made him collect every dropped pebble in the harbor basin, the same spatial-acoustic reading that his body did automatically in enclosed water. He oriented toward the sound. He began picking up the river stones from the underwater silt as he rose. He collected every single one. <!-- L0096 -->

He crawled up the muddy shore, covered in sediment and basin silt, goggles clear, holding a cupped double-handful of river stones, and glared over his shoulder at the tree line. <!-- L0096 -->

"The trees," he said, with the flat certainty of someone reporting a confirmed hazard. "They're hostile. They threw themselves at me." <!-- L0098 -->

"The *trees* didn't throw themselves, Iggy," Aggie said. "The cyclops threw *them.*" <!-- L0100 -->

Iggy considered this information. He did not find it meaningfully reassuring. He kept his eyes on the tree line.

## CHAPTER 92: THE NATTY RENAMING

Midnight in Sector 3. <!-- L0106 -->

The Canopy Raft was not yet a raft. It was a collection of intentions and raw materials spread across a section of cleared shoreline: six iron-hooped buoyancy barrels, coils of steel cable and high-tension bungee cord, two canvas tarp sections, a central burner fixture, fuel bladders sealed with dark tar, hollow bamboo tubes, coolant hoses, mycelial root bundles, several petrified seed pods, glowing bioluminescent moss harvested from the basin margin, and one large raw sulfur energy crystal that had been, in some technical sense, stolen.

Loami arrived first, hauling the barrel collection and copper fittings from the Sector 1 run. He handed Ignatious a wineskin as he dropped the load. <!-- L0108 -->

"First of all," Loami said, organizing barrels with one hand while taking a drink himself, "your name is great and all. But we already have an Iggy. We have an Aggie. So, you're now Natty." He pointed. "I'm calling you Natty." <!-- L0108 -->

Ignatious looked at him. His flame hair flickered at a mild to moderate outrage temperature. He appeared to be calculating whether this was a fight worth having.

It was not. <!-- L0108 -->

"Alright, crew," Loami said, surveying the haul. "Phase 1 is done. Now we build." <!-- L0110 -->

They built through the night. <!-- L0112 -->

**The Deck and Hull:** Six iron-hooped barrels bound with steel cables and high-tension bungee cord — Loami at the center of every connection, checking tension, adjusting load distribution, calling measurements to the rest of the crew. A heavy canvas deck stretched tight across the top of the barrel cluster, anchored with iron rivets that Loami had produced from somewhere on his person. Four heavy rusted shovels served as steering oars, lashed to the port and starboard edges at low angles. <!-- L0113 -->

**The Balloon Canopy:** Fuel bladders sealed with dark tar, stitched together in an overlapping pattern that Loami and Natty worked out by argument and trial across two hours. The canopy was painted in mottled green and brown using pigments Loami mixed from mineral deposits and harvested plant matter — functional camouflage, not decoration. The central burner fixture received the raw sulfur crystal from Iggy's earlier expedition. When lit, the crystal would heat the air inside the canopy with the particular energy-resonance of compressed sulfur, which was different in composition and efficiency from standard combustion. <!-- L0114 -->

**Armament:** Two heavy coolant-hose slingshots, mounted at the fore and aft of the deck. Ammunition: petrified seed pods wrapped in glowing green bioluminescent moss, creating tracer rounds visible at a hundred yards; mycelial root bundles for area suppression. Bamboo blow-dart launchers for close range. <!-- L0115 -->

At 2:00 AM, Loami stood back and looked at what they had built, ran the Crafting check against DC 15, and cleared it cleanly — full success. <!-- L0117 -->

"She's airtight, buoyant, and hidden," he said. <!-- L0117 -->

"She is," Natty confirmed, looking at the camouflaged balloon canopy with the proprietary pride of someone who had technically argued for a different design and lost and is prepared to acknowledge the correct outcome.

She was all three things. <!-- L0117 -->

## CHAPTER 93: RILL AT FOUR IN THE MORNING

At 4:00 AM, silver river fog rolled across the Sector 3 shoreline. <!-- L0123 -->

The squad had been taking rotating rest shifts between construction passes. Britt was awake. Aggie was awake. The raft sat complete on the shoreline, its camouflage canopy deflated and folded across the deck, its burner cold and waiting.

A figure stepped out of the mist.

She moved quietly — the particular quiet of someone who has learned to move through places without announcing themselves, which was a different skill from simply being soft-footed. Her blue hair was damp with fog. Her canvas captain's coat was dark with it. She stopped at the edge of the shoreline, looking at the Canopy Raft with an expression that was both evaluating and something warmer than evaluation. <!-- L0123 -->

"You've built something impressive out of scrap, candidates," Rill said. <!-- L0125 -->

"Rill!" Aggie took a step forward, surprised. "What are you doing out here?" <!-- L0127 -->

Rill's gaze moved from the raft to Aggie, then to the rest of the squad behind her. "Warning you." Her voice was quiet but not hushed — the tone of someone communicating information rather than atmosphere. "The 8:00 challenge isn't a standard run. The Dean and the Logistics Officers are dropping the sector suppression barriers." A pause. "Whatever is inside these sectors is coming out. If you're staying past the 5:00 AM evac deadline, survival itself is what gets you noticed." A ghost of something that might have been dry approval. "They love their heroes." <!-- L0129 -->

She reached into her coat and held out a heavy brass-bound flare gun. Aggie took it. <!-- L0131 -->

"If the storm takes the sky or you get pinned in the basin," Rill said, "fire this straight up." She held Aggie's gaze for a moment. "Don't die out here." <!-- L0133 -->

She stepped back into the mist. Within three seconds she was invisible. Within ten seconds she was gone.

Somewhere across the basin, the 5:00 AM evacuation bell rang. Its tone carried through the fog and down to the water and across the shoreline and up the canyon walls and out across 77,000 candidates, the significant majority of whom were on their way out. <!-- L0135 -->

Squad 907 stayed. <!-- L0135 -->

## CHAPTER 94: THE DAGGER SHARKS

8:00 AM. <!-- L0141 -->

The sirens came first — a sustained, high-pitched drone from the arena intercom towers that cut through the morning fog and lasted exactly ten seconds before it stopped. In the silence that followed, a sound came down from the sector walls: the deep, structural *crack* of suppression barriers releasing simultaneously across five sectors, a noise like a canyon finding a new shape.

Bruised-violet static arcs crawled down the colossal iron legs of the Walker-Core suppression anchors as they discharged their stored field energy. The morning air tasted of ozone. <!-- L0141 -->

"Push," Loami said. <!-- L0143 -->

They pushed. The Canopy Raft went down the shoreline ramp and into the basin water with a satisfying thud and float — it held its position on the surface, buoyancy correct, deck level, camouflage canopy ready to be inflated. They were 100 yards into open basin water when the first fin appeared.

Long. Chitinous. Bioluminescent at the edges — a pale violet stripe running the full length of the lateral line. Then another fin. Then a third and fourth and fifth, cutting through the foam in V-shaped wakes. <!-- L0143 -->

Twelve-foot Dagger Sharks. Armored carapace shells. Glowing violet lateral stripes that pulsed in patterns that Iggy recognized immediately, from underwater, in the lower trenches, years ago — energy tracking signatures. Living-resonance hunters. They could follow the biological frequency of a living creature through open water for a quarter-mile. <!-- L0143 -->

"Dagger sharks!" Iggy scrambled toward the center of the raft, his clay skin losing color. "*Dagger sharks!* They track living energy signatures — they took my friends in the lower trenches! We have to get off the water *NOW!*" <!-- L0145 -->

"I'm lighting the sulfur crystal!" Natty was already at the burner. "Get the balloon up!" <!-- L0147 -->

He activated Sparking Engines. The burner ignited. The sulfur crystal took the heat and began its resonance cascade — a contained flame-to-mineral energy transfer that was cleaner and faster than standard combustion and produced approximately twice the thermal output. <!-- L0149 -->

A 12-foot Dagger Shark leaped out of the foam directly beside the raft. It cleared the hull in a clean arc and its armored jaw clamped down on Natty's shoulder — 8 physical damage. He burned an armor slot. He did not let go of the burner. His shoulder was already healing from the fire-kin regeneration that came online whenever his core heat spiked, but the damage was real and the armor slot was gone. He held the burner steady. <!-- L0149 -->

"Hold the fire steady, Natty! I've got you!" Aggie placed her hands on the burner frame beside his and pushed her Wild Touch ability through the crystal — a direct botanical-resonance amplification that supercharged the sulfur burn by forty percent for the next thirty seconds. <!-- L0151 -->

The balloon canopy filled in a rush. The Canopy Raft lifted — ten feet — fifteen — and then the water-grab of the basin let go and they were airborne, rising toward the Sector 3 canopy line thirty feet above. Below them, Dagger Sharks snapped at the empty water where the hull had been. <!-- L0153 -->

"Distraction tactic!" Iggy called. "They're still tracking our bio-resonance! Aggie, Britt — fire the seed pods toward the forest bank!" <!-- L0161 -->

Aggie and Britt loaded the coolant-hose slingshots — seed pods wrapped in glowing bioluminescent moss, each one a small green-light package that would broadcast false bio-signatures on contact and impact. They fired them in sequence: *THWWACK-SNAP! SPLAAASH!* Two pods arced across a hundred yards of open air and hit the shoreline mudflats, shattering on impact and broadcasting bioluminescent glow across sixty square feet of mud. <!-- L0163 -->

The Dagger Shark pod turned as one and moved toward the shore. Every fin, simultaneously, angling toward the false signals. <!-- L0163 -->

"Grappling hook deployed!" Loami swung the rusted grappling hook in a wide arc overhead and released it toward the high canopy. It caught an eighty-foot petrified bough on the second try with a sharp metallic *CLANG* that echoed off the canyon walls. He hauled the steel cable taut. <!-- L0165 -->

Squad 907 secured the lines. The Canopy Raft swung into the elevated canopy space and stopped — hidden within the foliage, anchored to the branch overhead, elevated thirty feet above the basin and invisible from below. The camouflage worked. The mottled green-and-brown exterior disappeared against the petrified canopy in a way that would require either proximity or very good luck to break. <!-- L0165 -->

Below them, the basin was no longer empty. The 8:00 AM barrier drop had been effective. Things were moving through Sectors 1, 2, 4, and 5 that had not been visible before — shapes in the canopy, disturbances in the water, the distant sound of something very large moving through the Sector 1 canyons. <!-- L0141 -->

The raft swayed gently in the updraft from the steam vents to the east.

Squad 907 held position in the canopy. Elevated. Hidden. Safe, for now, from everything below. <!-- L0167 -->
