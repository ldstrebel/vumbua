# DON'T TOUCH MY BISCUITS

---

## CHAPTER 1: THE NIGHT OF EMBERS

Rain fell through the colossal leaves in heavy, rhythmic sheets, filling the petrified canopy with a sound like distant drumming. Fifty feet below, the forest floor was lost in twilight shadow.

High in the woven limbs of four ancient petrified giants, the Canopy Raft strained against its high-tension bungees. Ignatious leaned over the wooden gunwale, blowing a plume of grey ash through his nostrils. Faint orange embers danced along the cuffs of his heavy leather coat, fighting off the damp chill.

"Well," Ignatious grunted, adjusting the warhammer strapped across his back. "The landing went better than I thought."

Beside him, Loami unbolted the central winch casing. He wiped grease and rainwater from his brow with the back of a calloused hand, adjusting his damp woolen flat cap.

"Gotta say I'm shocked we made it up here at all," Loami said, his broad shoulders tensing as he checked the rig's balance. "We look a little like a ragtag crew, but here we are. Safe as we're gonna get."

A low, resonant thud vibrated through the petrified wood beneath their boots. Miles away, through gaps in the foliage, a jagged pillar of violet lightning hammered the central Mon. The air tasted sharply of copper and ozone.

Ignatious squinted toward the flash. "What's the purpose of all this? Is the Academy testing our adventurism, or is there a reason the sky keeps tryin' to melt the obelisk?"

Loami's expression darkened. Before entering the Academy, he had spent years servicing power conduits in Harmony, learning the temperamental pulse of Aether technology.

"They've lost control," Loami murmured, inspecting the hum of their craft's instruments. "The nodes broadcast a high-frequency pulse to keep canopy beasts at bay. But whatever hit the bay short-circuited the main line. The shields are down. Recovery time... eight increments, minimum."

He looked back at the party. "We've got three clocks tickin' against us now: how long we can survive out here, when the storm surge hits, and whether this raft holds together."

Ignatious ran a gloved hand along a thick, fibrous vine clinging to the petrified trunk. "Can I grab some of these vines for extra ropage? You can always use more rope."

"Hop off if you want," Loami said, scanning the dark canopy. "I'm gonna scout."

Ignatious swung his boot over the rail, stepping onto a wide, moss-slick bough. But as his weight shifted, the dark foliage rustled unnaturally. He froze, clutching the vine.

"No, no," Ignatious muttered, his eyes darting through the leaves. "There's always somethin' hidin' out there waitin' to snap at you when you get close. All right, guys... I'm grabbin' some vines, but keep your eyes open. You nevah know when you'll need more rope."

Loami crouched low on the bough, narrowing his dark eyes. His low-light vision pierced the rain-dappled twilight. Beneath the creeping vines, sleek, four-legged silhouettes prowled across the petrified bark—lean, cat-like predators perfectly camouflaged against the dark timber.

"Hey!" Loami hissed, dropping his center of gravity. "We've got feline creatures out on the branches! Natty, hurry back!"

Britt and Aggie scrambled up beside him, peering into the shadows. The creatures were built somewhere between a coyote and a mountain cat—territorial predators native to the upper canopy, known as Meowders.

"A coyobi? Interesting," Ignatious mused.

Britt drew her hunting knife, her green skin shimmering faintly in the dim light. "Oh yeah, we know those! They're in our forest back home—Meowders!"

"Seen what?" Ignatious asked, blinking. "What did you see?"

A sudden flurry of scurrying claws echoed across the bark. Dozens of glowing orange eyes ignited in the dark leaves surrounding the party.

At the rear of the raft, Iggy sat curled in a tight, shivering ball inside his heavy wool trench coat. His round copper goggles were slick with rain, his small clay-and-soil body rocking back and forth in sheer terror.

"The treeth have eyeth!" Iggy whimpered, clutching his head. "The treeth have eyeth!"

"Those aren't trees, Iggy!" Aggie called back. "They're basically just mean cats!"

Britt didn't hesitate. "Natty—move over, I've got a plan!" she shouted, vaulting past Ignatious.

She raised her hands and unleashed *Hypnotic Shimmer*. A brilliant cascade of pulsing chromatic light erupted across the bough. The radiant illusion dazzled the pack of Meowders, leaving them pawing blindly at the glowing air.

"Are they dazed?!" Ignatious called out.

"They're stunned!" Britt shouted back. "Move while they're dazed!"

"Do we try to move the raft or lower a branch?" Loami asked, eyeing their position.

"With lightning and thunder like this?" Ignatious countered. "Can we get away, or are these things gonna pursue us with bloodlust?"

"They're territorial," Aggie warned. "They won't back off easily."

"I'm gonna try somethin' else," Ignatious said. "Just brandishin' the flames a bit! Let's see how these cats like my dreadful visage!" Fanning the inner furnace of his chest, he activated *Dread Visage*. A terrifying, roaring face of orange fire erupted over his features.

Loami grinned, stepping beside him with his spanner raised. "All right, you little cats! Bring it on!"

The explosion of fire shattered the nerve of several Meowders. Screeching in terror, two of the creatures scrambled back into the foliage. But the alpha snarled, lunging forward with reckless fury, claws raking across Loami's shoulder pauldron.

"Psh, these little cats can only do minor damage!" Loami grunted, bracing as his armor absorbed the strike.

## CHAPTER 2: THE CAZITO MANEUVER

Rain drummed against the giant leaves in a steady rhythm.

Britt stepped to the edge of the limb, running her green-skinned hand along the petrified grain. "This forest feels wrong," she murmured to Aggie. "Fifty feet up, dead quiet... like a set built to imitate real life. And when I touch the bark, there's no hum. The Mizizi network is dead here."

Ahead on the bough, the remaining Meowders darted around Loami's boots. Loami swung a devastating right hook, but the cat twisted beneath his fist with improbable speed.

"Dammit! These skimpers are quick!" Loami spat as his fist whistled through empty air.

"I'll help speed you up!" Ignatious roared. Channeling his hearthfire, he pulsed a wave of invigorating energy into Loami's spirit. "Try again!"

Loami lunged again, but the tree-cats were too nimble, nipping at his collar.

Britt's eyes brightened. "Would it be possible to grab one of these vines and tie a lazizi? It's a traditional Mizizi hunting method!"

Ignatious turned to her in confusion, his hands still smoldering. "I have no idea what a 'Lazizi' is! But if you call it out, I'll shape it!"

"I think you'd call it a lasso!" Aggie called out, translating for Britt.

Nodding, Ignatious seized a thick length of raw vine, channeling *Shaped Material*. Flames licked along the fibers, instant-curing the wood into a supple, perfectly balanced hunting loop. He tossed it to Britt. "There! A flawless Lazizi rope!"

Loami stared in awe. "Holy shit, Natty, you're really good at that!" He drew upon *Soldier's Bond*, bolstering both himself and Ignatious as they stared down the agile felines.

Britt slapped a wide-brimmed cowboy hat onto her head. Channeling her *Hunter* training, she whirled the flame-forged vine above her head while Ignatious fortified her throw with a surge of prayer dice.

"Cazito! I got ya now!" Britt yelled as the loop sailed through the tempest wind. "Come here, you!"

The loop snapped tight around their necks, bundling the three Meowders together like a litter of house cats picked up by the scruff.

Britt planted her boots, spun the entire cluster around her head in a roaring arc, and pitched them off the bough into the fifty-foot abyss below.

"LAZIZI!" she screamed.

Loami howled with laughter, clapping Ignatious on the shoulder. "What a Kazizi-boy! Bye, gazitos!"

Ignatious blinked, wiping soot from his brow. "I don't know what any of these words mean!"

"It's Spanish!" Loami yelled back. "A gazito!"

"Yeah, that doesn't help," Ignatious replied, scratching his head as they turned back toward the raft.

## CHAPTER 3: THE STORM RAPTOR

As the Meowders vanished into the understory, a shrill screech echoed from above.

A massive shadow cut through the driving rain. A high-pitched, furious voice echoed through the upper canopy:

"NO! MY BISCUIT! NOT THESE! THESE ARE MINE, YOU FLAPPING FUZZBALL!" Pip screamed from above.

Loami's head snapped upward. "Is that Pip?!"

High above, tiny halfling legs were kicking frantically in mid-air. Pip was trapped in the talons of a monstrous Storm Raptor flying across the high branches.

"LET ME GO, YOU STUPID BIRD! DON'T TOUCH MY BISCUITS!" Pip screamed.

"I'm climbin' to the top of the canopy to get in range!" Loami shouted, scrambling up the slick bark.

The instant Aggie's boots touched the upper branch, the entire forest erupted in a rush of telepathic whispers: *"Aggie, Aggie, Aggie, Aggie!"*

Aggie closed her eyes, listening as the ancient roots poured impressions into her mind. She turned to the squad, translating the rustling plant-whispers. A 'little tree'—Bramble—had been using *Speak to Plants* to send signals through the network. He apologized through the arbol telephone network, explaining he had been seeding false signals to confuse rival salvagers.

"That son of a bitch!" Ignatious swore fondly.

Aggie's eyes widened as the vision clarified. Bramble had realized Squad 907 was in the forest and was trying to reach them because Pip had run out alone before the storm to fetch emergency breakfast pastries!

Above them, the Storm Raptor carrying Pip circled closer.

On the raft deck, hearing Pip's screams shattered Iggy's paralysis instantly. "I have to help her!" he shouted, surging to his feet and raising his wooden staff. "Eth-ereal Wall!"

A fifty-foot barrier of shimmering translucent force erupted in mid-air. The Storm Raptor screeched, slamming full-force into the wall and beating its wings weakly against the barrier.

"Stupid bird! Watch where you're flyin'! You're gonna make me drop my biscuits!" Pip yelled, clutching her canvas pouch as golden crumbs rained down.

Ignatious squinted into the rain, muttering to himself, "Hmm... are English biscuits or nice southern biscuits?!"

Hesitating only a second as the bird hung stalled against the barrier, Ignatious raised his hand and fired a bolt of pure flame at the Raptor. "This bird glows! It's vulnerable! I saved you! You're welcome! I love it when a plan comes together!"

The impact forced the hawk's talons open, dropping Pip into open air. But as she fell, three bioluminescent shadows bounded out of the foliage—Mwaza-Chui, apex canopy jaguars lured by the rich scent of warm butter and caramelized bacon.

## CHAPTER 4: THE TREE IGLOO

Loami sprinted toward the drop zone. "Hey! You want a piece of this?! Come and get a little piece of this!"

Two jaguars darted past him, while the third leapt and caught Pip by her ponytail in its massive jaws, dangling her over the edge!

"How many god's damn cats are in this gods damn forest!" Ignatious bellowed.

The jaguar opened its jaws and unleashed *Frequency Overload*—a roaring cone of static ember energy that rocked Loami and Iggy, suppressing their spellcasting under a wave of psychic shock.

Pip dangled over the abyss, smacking the predator's snout with her food bag. "These are my biscuits! Stop it!"

Suddenly, the tempest peaked. The colossal trees bent in half under a howling hurricane gale that battered the canopy with sleet and hail.

Reacting instantly, Ignatious used *Shaped Material* to form a wooden shield against the sleet. Aggie ran beneath the limb using *Wall Walk*, placed her palm against the bark, and channeled *Wild Fortress*, growing a complete Tree Igloo of living root-vines around Pip and Loami.

"Loami, get into the shell! I can protect you from the storm!" Aggie shouted through the wind.

Loami dived over Pip, rolling them both into the wooden dome like a player recovering a fumble, just as the root-vines sealed tight.

Inside the quiet Tree Igloo, Pip gasped, "I just wanted to get some snacks, man!"

"I know you did, Pip! That does sound about right," Loami replied, catching his breath.

Leaning back against the root wall, Loami looked over at Pip. "So here's my theory: I think they created this challenge for first-years, and somethin' went terribly wrong and short-circuited. Are you familiar with the Day of Sparks?"

"It's the Night of Sparks!" Pip corrected chidingly, opening her bag of snacks to inspect the damage. "Did you not listen to anythin' they taught us in school?!"

"Uh, no," Loami admitted. "But the Night of Sparks—that was about havin' too much power. It was too little power here! The shields went down, and all these beasts started stormin' the ring!"

Outside, through the gale, stadium speakers crackled across the sky: *Just hold on... halfway... shield crack up... hold on!*

In the bay below, a giant iron steamship—the *Deep-Hull*—rose through the waves, its heavy steam cannons firing pulse rounds to push back beasts attacking the center spire.

---

## CHAPTER 5: THE RAFT DROP

Outside the Igloo, as the storm wall passed, Ignatious looked at his boots, then up into the sky, before slapping his own forehead in sudden realization.

"Wait a minute," Ignatious realized aloud. "I can fucking fly!"

Ignatious ignited his ember wings, soaring up with his warhammer to strike the glowing Storm Raptor, sending it crashing into the underbrush.

Meanwhile, the pack of Mwaza-Chui slammed against the exterior of the Tree Igloo, their claws punching at the timber. Within, Pip and Loami sat in comfort, scarfing down a biscuit each.

"This biscuit is great, I can feel the stress drainin' away!" Loami exclaimed between bites.

"Yeah, I'm nevah stressed!" Pip replied through a mouthful of buttery flakes.

Suddenly, a jaguar snout breached the dome, roaring into the igloo.

"Pip, watch out!" Loami yelled, jumping to cover her. But Pip simply dodged.

"Bad cat!" Pip scolded, popping up and bopping the jaguar on the nose with her bag of food. The jaguar froze, scrunching its nose in shock.

Outside, Britt and Iggy gathered a massive pile of phosphorescent mushrooms along the trunk. "In Mizizi lore, jaguars are super attracted to bioluminescent light! I have a plan," Britt said, collecting spores while Iggy stacked them on the raft deck.

Aggie channeled *Conjure Swarm*, sending a trail of glowing fireflies toward the bait. The scent and brilliant glow caught the jaguars' nostrils. Turning away from the Igloo, the pack bounded onto the raft deck to gorge on the fungi.

Pip started to yell, "Hey, you big furbag—!" before Aggie shushed her.

As the cats gorged, Loami stalked behind them. Waiting until all three were on the deck, Loami heaved his shoulder against the timber. "Now! Push together and cut the bungees!" Loami yelled.

Britt slashed the high-tension bungees. Loami and Iggy heaved with all their strength, tipping the Canopy Raft off the branch.

The raft pitched over, sending the entire pack of Mwaza-Chui plummeting fifty feet into the dark abyss below with echoing roars and a heavy crash.

Across the branch, a second Storm Raptor lunged at Iggy.

"Natty, bring it this way!" Loami shouted across the canopy.

Ignatious dodged a talon strike and streaked across the bough. As the Raptor dived, Loami stepped in, shielding Ignatious with a well-timed punch that countered the talons. Frustrated and outnumbered, the Raptor screeched and fled into the storm.

"Oh my gods, thank you!" Ignatious gasped.

"I figured a little one-on-one with one of those things wasn't a fair fight," Loami said.

"The one-on-one was fine. The two-on-one aftahward was not!" Ignatious laughed.

## CHAPTER 6: THE SACRED TORTOISE

The wind died down to a light, cool sprinkle. Sitting across the bough was Pip, gnawing on her jerky.

Behind her sat Mwaza-Kasa—the sacred Spirit Tortoise of the Mizizi clan, its bark-like shell looming over Pip, its glassy eyes flicking between Aggie and Britt.

"Is that... a family membah of yours?" Ignatious whispered.

"It's just sittin' there loomin'... menacingly," Loami noted.

Britt gestured frantically for Pip to back away, but Pip reached into her bag.

"I found a big turtle!" Pip announced, holding out a biscuit. The tortoise snapped it down in one bite. "See, it's my friend!" Pip exulted, scratching under its chin.

"Don't—!" Britt gasped.

The moment Pip touched its chin, a deep *BOOM* echoed as the tortoise collapsed, its eyes glowing ember-red. Pip's eyes rolled back, and she collapsed down the branch.

Aggie sprinted forward, catching Pip before she slipped off the edge. Pip was already convulsing in her arms—entering the mycelium network alone.

"You can nevah go through the network alone, or you get pulled in and nevah come out! How do we anchor her?!" Britt cried.

"Touching Mwaza-Kasa is taboo to the Mizizi, but in absence of the trees, it acts as a physical anchor!" Aggie realized. "We have to touch the living sacred tortoise to save Pip!"

Britt grabbed Aggie's hand. "Three... two... one!"

Pip immediately calmed into a peaceful sleep in Aggie's arms. Simultaneously, Aggie and Britt slumped forward against the shell, entering the deep network.

Ignatious blinked. "Hey, Loami! What's with the weird glowin' turtle?! Everyone who touches that turtle falls asleep! Am I the crazy one here?! We should NOT touch that turtle!"

"I think it might be a little too late for that," Loami replied sardonically, stepping forward with his hand outstretched.

## CHAPTER 7: WHAT WAS FORGOTTEN

Touching Mwaza-Kasa plunged Aggie and Britt into the deep network. They found Pip easily, lost in a vast, silent forest.

As they reached her side, the network unlocked a forgotten memory:

Young child versions of Aggie and Britt were laughing as they ran through a sunlit sacred grove—a memory wiped from their minds years ago. In the memory, the children sat on a mossy root to rest. But as adult Aggie and Britt watched, they saw the root open its eyes. It was a Spirit Tortoise. The moment the children touched it, they passed out, and the vision shattered.

Memories played across the bark like moving shadows: they remembered lost clan members who had vanished years ago after dying of the green shell rot currently on Britt's arm.

In their adult bodies, Britt and Aggie grabbed Pip's arms and pulled.

Loami was inches from touching the shell when Britt, Aggie, and Pip's eyes snapped open back on the high branch, gasping for breath!

"Hey! Whoa! What happened?!" Loami asked.

"You saw that?!" Aggie gasped, staring at Britt.

"Yeah... I definitely saw that," Britt said, shaken. "We were kids... in the sacred grove..."

"What?! Saw what?!" Ignatious asked.

"WHAT THE SHIT WAS THAT, MR TURTLE!?" Pip screamed, glaring at the tortoise.

Mwaza-Kasa stood slowly on four legs, looking sad and determined. It stomped its heavy foot twice against the bark, tucked its limbs into its shell, and rolled off the edge of the fifty-foot branch!

"OH NO!" Iggy yelled.

*CRASH!*

The shell plunged fifty feet, smashing into the ground with a thud that created a dirt crater. Moments later, the tortoise popped out of its shell, looked up at Squad 907, and waited.

"Well... that can't be right!" Loami said.

"I think it wants you to follow it!" Ignatious said.

"Well, I'm definitely not jumpin' off the tree!" Britt said.

"I can carry one of you down!" Ignatious offered.

Loami shrugged. "I've fallen from furthah! Besides... I'm nevah stayin' up in these trees alone!"

Loami dived fifty feet straight down into the crater. Slamming into the dirt, he staggered to his feet and bowed deeply to Mwaza-Kasa. The sacred tortoise nodded in approval.

Britt used *Wall Walk* to carry Pip down, who grumbled, "My headdd." Iggy walked over and hand-fed Pip bacon biscuits as she cried tears of relief.

Mwaza-Kasa looked at Squad 907, nodded slowly, and turned to lead them on foot into the petrified forest as dawn broke.
